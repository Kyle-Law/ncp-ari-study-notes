Picking single-mode optics by distance

On single-mode fiber, distance decides the optic. Each step up in reach changes how the lanes travel (parallel fibers or several wavelengths on one pair), which connector you need, and how much loss the link can tolerate.

Exam objectiveDescribe the transceiver requirements across the various SMF distances.

In one minute

  • The reach ladder: DR ≈ 500 m → FR ≈ 2 km → LR ≈ 10 km → ER ≈ 40 km. All run at about 1310 nm on OS2 fiber.
  • DR4 is parallel: 4 lanes on 4 fiber pairs (8 fibers), using an MPO-12 connector, which is APC in NVIDIA's NDR family. FR4/LR4 are WDM: 4 wavelengths muxed onto one duplex pair, usually LC.
  • Every reach class has a loss budget (max channel insertion loss). For 400G it's roughly 3 dB for DR4, 4 dB for FR4 and 6.3 dB for LR4. Fiber, connectors and splices all spend that budget.
  • PAM4 signalling is sensitive to reflections, which is why angled (APC) end-faces and clean connectors matter so much.
  • Short and long both fail: too much loss starves the receiver, but a long-reach optic on a very short link can overload it unless you add an attenuator.

The reach ladder

DR≈ 500 m

Parallel single-mode. One wavelength (1310 nm), one fiber per lane per direction. 400G DR4 = 4 × 100G on 8 fibers through an MPO-12. NVIDIA's twin-port 800G optics carry two of these (2 × DR4 → two MPO-12s). Default choice for in-hall AI fabric links.

FR≈ 2 km

WDM on a duplex pair. Four CWDM wavelengths around 1271–1331 nm are multiplexed onto one fiber each way, using duplex LC. Fewer fibers, longer reach, pricier optics. Good for building-to-building or large campus halls.

LR≈ 10 km

WDM, higher budget. Same idea as FR4 with better lasers and receivers. Used for campus and metro links between data halls.

ER≈ 40 km

Extended reach. Rare inside an AI cluster. NVIDIA's validated list includes a single 40 km ER module for long interconnects.

NVIDIA's single-mode (MMS…) parts in the ConnectX-7 validated list cover 100 m, 500 m, 2 km (FR4), 10 km (LR4) and one 40 km ER module. Check the list for the exact part and firmware.

Exam traps

  • "More reach is always safer."No. LR optics on a few metres of fiber can overload the receiver. Use the reach class that fits, or add an attenuator.
  • DR4 needs 8 fibers, FR4 needs 2.Patch panels and trunks have to provide the right fiber count and connector (MPO-12 vs duplex LC).
  • Connectors eat the budget fastest.On a short DR4 link, four dirty or poor connectors can blow a 3 dB budget before the fiber itself matters.
  • APC vs UPC.NVIDIA's NDR single-mode cables are MPO-12/APC. Mating APC to UPC damages end-faces and causes high loss and reflection.
  • Wavelength.Data-center SMF optics run around 1310 nm. 850 nm is multimode.

Check yourself

1. Two halls are 1.5 km apart, with only a few spare fiber pairs between them. Which 400G optic fits?

1.5 km is past DR4's ~500 m. FR4 reaches ~2 km and needs only one duplex pair per link.

2. How many fibers does a 400G DR4 link use, and on which connector?

4 transmit + 4 receive fibers, carried in an MPO-12 with 4 positions unused.

3. What does the "4" in FR4 mean?

FR4 uses 4 CWDM wavelengths on a duplex pair. DR4 is the parallel one.

4. A 300 m DR4 link fails its loss test. What's the most likely culprit?

300 m of OS2 costs about 0.12 dB. Connectors at 0.5 dB+ each (more when dirty) are what consume a 3 dB budget.
