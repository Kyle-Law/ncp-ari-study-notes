A 9-micron window

NVIDIA's guide to maintaining NDR InfiniBand cables boils down to one rule: clean the fiber connector and the transceiver port every single time you plug in. This page explains why, with pictures.

NDR (400 Gb/s InfiniBand) leans much more heavily on optical links than earlier generations. Light travels through a glass core only about 9 micrometres wide — a fraction of the width of a human hair — so a speck of dust can block it.

What the page says, in plain terms

Every optical cable and transceiver ships with dust caps over the connector faces. Those caps protect the glass until installation, but once they come off, the faces are exposed.

Before inserting a cable, you clean two things: the MPO connector on the cable and the receptacle inside the transceiver. You do this on every insertion, not just the first one. If a cable gets pulled out by accident, it gets cleaned again before it goes back in.

Skipping this step can degrade the optical signal enough to lose data. The guide shows microscope photos of a connector that picked up dirt after a single uncleaned insertion, and had its core fully covered after the second.

It closes by contrasting a grimy MPO connector face with a clean one, and points to a dedicated MPO fiber cleaner (a click-style pen cleaner) as the tool for the job.

The rule: cleaned, then plugged — every time, on both the cable end and the port.

Why 9 µm is so unforgiving

The glass fiber has two parts: a core that carries the light and a cladding around it that keeps the light in. In single-mode fiber the core is about 9 µm across. Common dust particles are in the same size range or larger, so one particle landing in the centre can block most of the light.

Where the cleaning happens: the MPO connector

NDR optical cables end in MPO connectors — a rectangular ferrule holding a row of fibers side by side, lined up by two guide pins. The cable's MPO plugs into a receptacle in the OSFP or QSFP112 transceiver. Dirt on either face ends up sandwiched between them, so both sides need cleaning.

The procedure, as a loop

The important part isn't the individual steps — it's that the loop restarts whenever a connector comes out, planned or not.

A practical habit many teams add (not stated on this page, but standard fiber practice): inspect with a fiber scope after cleaning, and put dust caps back on anything left unplugged.

In one paragraph

NDR InfiniBand relies heavily on optical cables with MPO connectors. The light-carrying core of each fiber is about 9 µm wide, so even tiny dust particles can block it. Take the dust caps off, clean the cable's MPO end-face and the transceiver's receptacle with a proper MPO cleaner, then plug in — and repeat that cleaning every time a cable is reconnected, including after an accidental unplug. Otherwise you risk a degraded signal and lost data.
