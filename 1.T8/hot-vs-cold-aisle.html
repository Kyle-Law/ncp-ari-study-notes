Hot aisle vs. cold aisle

Every server breathes the same way: cool air in at the front, hot air out at the back. Hot/cold aisle layout lines the racks up so fronts face fronts and backs face backs. Containment then walls one of the two aisles off so the air streams can't mix.

Exam objectiveDifferentiate between hot-aisle and cold-aisle cooling.

In one minute

  • Layout: alternate rows so intakes share a cold aisle (perforated tiles or overhead diffusers here) and exhausts share a hot aisle (returns here). This alone saves roughly 10–35% cooling energy.
  • Cold aisle containment (CAC): roof and end doors enclose the cold aisle. The rest of the room becomes hot. Easier to retrofit on raised floors.
  • Hot aisle containment (HAC): enclose the exhaust side and duct it straight back to the cooling units (often via a ceiling plenum). The room stays cool. Captures heat more completely and is usually the more effective option, supporting higher density.
  • Either way: blanking panels, sealed floor cut-outs, no solid rack doors, and control cooling from server inlet temperature (ASHRAE recommended ~18–27 °C).
  • Two failure modes containment fixes: recirculation (hot exhaust back into intakes) and bypass (cold air returning without touching a server).

Density and PUE ranges are the typical figures from the Legrand article (1 · SR11). CRAC with no containment: ~3–5 kW per rack, PUE ~1.8–2.2. Dense AI racks go well beyond air and need liquid (see 1 · T6).

Make either one work

Blanking panels

Fill every empty U and the gaps between racks, or exhaust sneaks forward to the intakes.

Seal the floor

Grommets on cable cut-outs, sealed tile joints and perimeter. Perforated tiles only in the cold aisle, placed by CFD.

No solid doors

Solid glass or Plexiglas doors deflect air. Use perforated doors.

Control on inlet temp

Measure server inlet (low, middle, high on each rack) and control cooling from it, not from return air. Hold about 18–27 °C.

Clear underfloor & rear

Remove abandoned cable. Don't let rear cable bundles choke exhaust.

Raise the setpoint

With containment, supply air can move toward the top of the ASHRAE range, saving chiller energy.

Exam traps

  • "Hot aisle containment makes the room hot."It's the reverse. HAC keeps the room cool. CAC makes the room the hot return.
  • Perforated tiles in the hot aisle.That's bypass air. Tiles belong in the cold aisle only.
  • Controlling from return temperature.With containment, return air is much hotter. Control from server inlet temperature.
  • Containment without blanking panels.Open U-spaces let air short-circuit through the rack.
  • Layout vs containment.Hot/cold aisle is the arrangement. Containment is the physical barrier added on top.

Check yourself

1. With cold aisle containment, what temperature is the general room?

CAC encloses the supply aisle, so everything outside it carries exhaust back to the cooling units.

2. Which containment usually captures heat more completely and supports higher density?

HAC ducts exhaust straight back to the cooling units, so less mixing and higher return temperatures.

3. Hot exhaust curling over rack tops into server intakes is called…

Recirculation = exhaust back into intakes. Bypass = cold air returning without passing through equipment.

4. Where should perforated floor tiles go?

Supply air should arrive exactly where servers draw it in.

5. What is the ASHRAE recommended range for server inlet air?

18–27 °C (64.4–80.6 °F) is the commonly cited ASHRAE recommended envelope for IT inlet air.
