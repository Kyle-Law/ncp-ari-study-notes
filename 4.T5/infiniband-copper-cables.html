Deploying NDR/XDR copper cables

Copper is the cheapest, lowest-latency, lowest-power way to connect a port, but only over a couple of metres. It's also thick, heavy and stiff, so a good copper install is mostly about choosing the right length and protecting the connector from the cable's own weight.

Exam objectiveDeploy InfiniBand NDR/XDR copper cables.

In one minute

  • Passive DAC (MCP…): about 0.5–3 m at NDR. Active copper / ACC (MCA…): about 4–6.5 m. Beyond that, use AOC or transceivers with fiber. At XDR's 200G lanes, copper reach shrinks further, so check the datasheet.
  • Plan every run under 90% of the cable's rated reach, and match the ends: finned twin-port OSFP on the switch, flat-top on adapters/DGX, splitters for 2 × 400G or 4 × 200G.
  • Copper is roughly 9.4 mm thick and 0.045 kg/m, about 10× the tray area and 5× the weight of an AOC. Support it at least every 2 m with hook-and-loop ties every 30–60 cm, and never let it hang from the port.
  • Install by the book: velcro the cable to the rack, pre-bend the tip, grip the connector body and push square until it latches, then install one, test one.
  • Keep copper away from EMI sources (power cords, PDUs, converters, fluorescent lights) and in separate runs from fiber.

Copper's trade-offs

Why choose it

  • Cheapest per link
  • Passive DAC draws essentially no power: no optics to cool
  • Lowest latency (no electrical-optical conversion)
  • Nothing to clean: no fiber end-faces

What it costs you

  • Short reach, and shorter with every lane-speed jump (PAM4 levels sit closer together)
  • Thick and stiff: large bend radius, hard to dress in dense racks
  • Heavy: ~5× an AOC per metre, so loads trays and connectors
  • Picks up EMI

Install it right

  1. Verify before you open the bagPart number, length and end types against the cable plan and labels. Unpack gently with ESD precautions. Copper cables with active ends (ACC) contain electronics.
  2. Plan the sideSplit each switch's cables 50/50 between the left and right managers, and plug from the edges toward the centre so the middle stays clear for fan and PSU removal.
  3. Mount, then pre-bendFix the cable to the rack with hook-and-loop first, then shape a wide curve near the plug toward its route. This matters most for thick 26 AWG copper. The port should carry no leverage.
  4. Insert by the bodyGrip the connector body between thumb and index finger, hold it square to the panel and push until it latches. Never push via the cable, and never use the pull-tab to insert.
  5. Install one, test oneConnect both ends and confirm a physical link (port LED) and a logical link before the next cable. A bad cable is cheap to find now and expensive inside a finished bundle.
  6. Support and dressSupports at ≤ 2 m (≤ 60 cm in the SuperPOD dressing guidance), hook-and-loop every 30–60 cm, snug but not deforming the jacket. Keep away from EMI sources, out of fiber runs, and off other equipment's vents.
  7. Remove by the tabPull the tab straight out, perpendicular to the panel, until the latch releases. Never pull on the jacket.

Verifying the link

InfiniBand reports two states per port: physical (is there a working signal on the cable?) and logical (has the Subnet Manager configured the port?). The cable is your job, the SM is the fabric's.

LinkUpPhysical state: signal locked, cable OK
Polling / DisabledPhysical state: no partner or bad cable
DownLogical state
InitializingLogical: waiting for the Subnet Manager
ActiveLogical: SM has configured the port; traffic flows

Also useful: iblinkinfo (every link's width and speed across the fabric), mlxlink -d <device> (speed, FEC, BER, cable/module info on the host), and the switch port LED. A link that comes up at a lower rate or width than expected usually means a damaged or wrong cable.

Exam traps

  • "Use the longest DAC that fits."No. Choose the shortest length that routes cleanly, keep runs under 90% of rated reach, and tuck limited slack into the managers.
  • Pushing by the pull-tab.The tab is only for extraction. Insert by the connector body, square to the panel.
  • Physical LinkUp but State Initializing.That's not a cable fault. The port is waiting for the Subnet Manager.
  • Copper beside power.Route copper away from PDUs, power cords and converters. EMI raises errors.
  • Letting a heavy DAC hang.Never more than 2 m unsupported. The cable's weight becomes strain on the port.

Check yourself

1. A switch-to-switch run inside the same rack is 2 m. Most cost-effective NDR choice?

2 m is well within passive DAC reach (~0.5–3 m at NDR): cheapest, no power, lowest latency.

2. A run is 5 m. Which copper option, if any?

ACC stretches copper to roughly 4–6.5 m at NDR. Beyond that, use AOC or optics.

3. After cabling, ibstat shows Physical state LinkUp, State Initializing. What next?

LinkUp means the cable and signal are fine. Initializing means the port hasn't been configured by an SM yet.

4. Correct way to insert a copper cable into an OSFP cage?

NVIDIA's guidance is to grip the body near its lower part, hold it square to the panel and push gently. The tab is for removal only.

5. Why does the 50/50 left/right split matter more for copper than for AOC?

At 9.4 mm and 0.045 kg/m, copper fills managers fast. Splitting it keeps both sides serviceable and the centre clear.
