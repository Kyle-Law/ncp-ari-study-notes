Reading a code designation

Every entry in the list is identified the same way. Once you can read one, you can read all 300+.

Suffixes group families together: 13, 13D and 13R are all sprinklers; 30, 30A and 30B are all flammable liquids; 70, 70B and 70E are all electrical. The edition year matters because a jurisdiction may legally enforce an older edition than the newest one in the catalog.

Four kinds of documents in one list

The list mixes documents with different levels of force. The word in the title tells you which kind you are looking at.

Code shall

Broad, mandatory requirements written so a government can adopt them into law. Example: NFPA 1 Fire Code, NFPA 70 National Electrical Code.

Standard shall

Mandatory requirements on a narrower subject, typically referenced by a code. Example: NFPA 13 on installing sprinklers.

Recommended practice should

Advice written in non-mandatory "should" language. Example: NFPA 70B was long a recommended practice before becoming a standard.

Guide informative

Explanatory material with no mandatory force. Example: NFPA 921, the guide for fire and explosion investigations.

How the documents work together

No single document covers a building. A fire code or building code sets the overall rules, then points to specialist standards for each system, which in turn point to their own inspection and maintenance standards. NFPA 1 alone references well over a hundred other NFPA documents.

Read left to right: the umbrella codes say what a building needs; the middle column says how to design and install it; the right column says how to inspect, test and maintain it for the life of the building.

Why each code has an edition year

Every document is owned by a technical committee and revised on a cycle of roughly three to five years through an open, consensus-based process. Around 10,000 volunteer committee members take part. Anyone can submit input.

  1. Public inputAnyone proposes changes to the current edition.
  2. First draftThe committee meets, acts on each input and ballots the result.
  3. Public commentThe first draft is published and the public comments on it.
  4. Second draftThe committee responds to comments and ballots again.
  5. Technical meetingUnresolved motions can be debated and voted on by NFPA members.
  6. Standards CouncilIssues the new edition, which then joins the catalog.
