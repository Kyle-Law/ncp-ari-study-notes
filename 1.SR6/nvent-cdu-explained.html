The coolant distribution unit

A plain-language walkthrough of nVent's CDU page: what a CDU is, how heat moves through it, what's inside, and which type fits which data center.

Facility water supply (cool) Facility water return (warmed) TCS coolant to chips TCS coolant back from chips (warm)

nVent's one-line definition: a CDU pumps liquid at the desired temperature directly onto the chip or server, and in doing so it controls three things — pressure, flow and temperature. nVent calls it the "heart" of a liquid‑cooled data center; elsewhere it adds "brain", because it also runs the control logic.

The key idea is the two separate loops above. nVent's Row CDU is fed from the primary facility water system, its built-in pumps drive the secondary technology cooling system loop, and a heat exchanger transfers the excess heat from the secondary coolant to the primary. The dirty, variable building water never touches the servers; the servers get clean, precisely conditioned coolant.

Why use liquid at all?

Modern AI chips produce far more heat than fans can carry away. nVent states that liquid has a heat‑transfer capacity roughly 3,500 times higher than air, because it is so much denser.

The grid on the right makes that concrete: every grey dot is a unit volume of air; the single blue dot is the same volume of water. That one blue dot carries about as much heat as all the grey ones together.

Moving heat with less fan energy is what lets CDUs lower PUE (power usage effectiveness — total facility power divided by IT power; closer to 1.0 is better) and raise how much computing fits in each rack.

  1 unit of water  ≈  3,500 units of air (70 × 50 dots). Ratio as stated by nVent; it compares heat capacity by volume.

Two ways to get rid of the heat

Every CDU collects heat from the chips, but it has to hand that heat to something. nVent's page frames the choice around what your building already has.

Liquid‑to‑liquid (L2L)

  • Needs a facility water system already piped to the white space.
  • Built for scale: nVent quotes 800 kW+ of heat rejection for its high‑capacity L2L units.
  • Best for new AI/HPC halls and full liquid‑cooled deployments; pairs well with free cooling.

Liquid‑to‑air (L2A)

  • No facility water needed: fans push the captured heat into the room, and the existing air handlers remove it.
  • nVent's LTA Sidecar supports up to two racks of liquid‑cooled equipment, and supports GB200 NVL36 in 1:1 configurations.
  • Best for retrofits: bringing a few liquid‑cooled AI racks into an air‑cooled hall with minimal building work.

Where a CDU sits

Seen from above, a data hall row. nVent offers CDUs at each scale: inside a rack, beside a pair of racks, and at the end of a whole row.

The nVent lineup, by scale

Rack CDU (AC and DC versions) sits in the rack and serves just that rack — the simplest way to add liquid to one high‑density cabinet.

LTA Sidecar stands beside up to two racks and dumps heat into room air; no facility water required.

Row CDU (RackChiller) is fed by facility water and supplies a whole row through manifolds; nVent's CDU800 family is built around reliability, availability and serviceability for direct‑to‑chip cooling.

Project Deschutes Open CDU is a high‑pressure L2L unit built to Google's Project Deschutes 5.0 spec, contributed to the Open Compute Project, and aimed at GB200/GB300‑class and TPU deployments.

For scale: 2 MW is the heat of roughly 700 electric kettles (3 kW each) boiling at once. The Open CDU also moves up to 500 gpm, uses N+1 seal‑less pumps and runs at a 3 °C approach temperature (how close the coolant gets to the facility water temperature). nVent says it offers up to 5× the performance of its previous rack CDU generations.

The CDU is one part of a system

Manifolds carry coolant from the CDU to the equipment in each rack; nVent's RackChiller manifolds use plug‑and‑play connections. Rear door heat exchangers (RDHx) cool the air leaving a rack using the same liquid; the RDHx PRO handles racks up to 78 kW. Heat rejection units cover sites without facility water. nVent also pairs its new CDUs and power distribution units on a shared control platform, so operators monitor cooling and power in one place.

Choosing: have facility water and big loads → row L2L CDU. No facility water, a few AI racks → LTA Sidecar. One dense rack → rack CDU. Hyperscale, OCP‑style racks → Deschutes Open CDU.
