Ecodesign rules for servers and data storage, explained

Commission Regulation (EU) 2019/424 sets minimum energy performance and repair-friendliness rules that most enterprise servers and online storage systems must meet before they can be sold in the EU. Here is what it requires, when, and how it's checked.

Efficiency, repair, data deletion and information rules + firmware availability + tighter PSU limits

Based on the text published in the Official Journal, L 74, 18 March 2019, pp. 46–66. Legal instruments get amended; check EUR-Lex for the current consolidated version before relying on this for compliance.

1What it covers

Article 1 (scope) and Article 2 (definitions)

Two product groups are in scope: servers and online data storage products. A server here is a machine that serves client devices over a network (not via keyboard and mouse), runs server operating systems or hypervisors, supports error‑correcting or buffered memory, and lets all its processors share memory under a single OS. An online data storage product is a complete storage system that delivers random‑access data with a time to first data under 80 ms.

Nine kinds of product are carved out, mostly because they are niche, highly specialised, or already covered elsewhere:

2Why it exists

Recitals 4–7

The preparatory study found two environmental issues worth regulating: electricity use while running, and resource efficiency (repair, reuse, upgrade and recycling). Projected consumption for 2030 is large once you count the cooling and backup power that each watt of IT load drags along with it.

4Power supply efficiency

Annex II points 1.1.1 and 1.1.2, Tables 1 and 2

Every AC‑powered server and storage product must use power supply units (PSUs) that convert mains power efficiently across the load range, not only at the sweet spot. Efficiency is checked at 10 %, 20 %, 50 % and 100 % of rated load, and power factor at 50 % load. The 2023 tier raised the bar for multi‑output units and added a 10 % load point for single‑output units, since servers often idle at low load for long periods.

5Idle state power

Annex II point 2.1, Tables 3 and 4 (servers with one or two sockets)

Idle means the OS has loaded and the server could do work, but nothing is requested. Servers spend a lot of time here, so the regulation caps idle power. 6Active state efficiency

Annex II point 2.2, Table 5; Annex III point 3

Idle power alone could reward a slow server. Active state efficiency measures useful work per watt while the machine is busy. The test runs eleven small workloads ("worklets") that stress the CPU, memory and storage; each gets an efficiency score of 1 000 × performance ÷ power. These are rolled up in three steps using geometric means, so no single worklet can dominate the result.

Weighting of the three workload types in the final score, with the worklets that make up each.

Effserver = exp[ 0.65 · ln(EffCPU) + 0.30 · ln(EffMemory) + 0.05 · ln(EffStorage) ]

The three sub‑scores are themselves geometric means: of 7 worklets for the CPU, 2 for memory, 2 for storage. The measured score must reach the minimum for its type:

7Repair, data deletion and firmware

Annex II point 1.2; Recitals 8–12

These circular‑economy rules aim to keep hardware in use longer by making it easier for refurbishers, repairers and recyclers to work on it, without compromising data protection under the GDPR.

Components that must be removable

Joining, fastening or sealing techniques must not prevent these from being taken apart for repair or reuse (when present):

Secure data deletion

A built‑in way to wipe all data from every storage device must be provided, overwriting it so recovery becomes infeasible for a given level of effort. It could live in the BIOS/firmware, on a bootable disc or USB stick shipped with the product, or in software for the supported operating systems. Manufacturers must explain how to use it and which deletion standards it supports.

How long firmware must stay available

Example: a model first sold in 2021 and last sold in 2025. The latest firmware version may be free or offered at a fair, transparent, non‑discriminatory cost; security updates must be free.

12The remaining articles

Articles 4, 6, 8, 9 and 10

Conformity assessment (Article 4)

Manufacturers self‑certify through internal design control or a management system (Annexes IV or V of the Ecodesign Directive 2009/125/EC), with the technical documentation containing the information in Annex II point 3.4.

Handover from Regulation 617/2013 (Article 9)

Computer servers used to fall under the general computers regulation. From 1 March 2020 they were removed from it to avoid overlap, leaving only small‑scale servers there.

Review (Article 8)

By March 2022 the Commission had to assess whether to:

  • tighten the idle power and active efficiency limits, and revise definitions and scope;
  • add information on more critical raw materials (tantalum, gallium, dysprosium, palladium) for recyclers;
  • exempt appliances, large, fully fault tolerant and network servers, and exclude resilient, HPC and integrated‑APA servers from the energy limits;
  • set new requirements on processor power management, operating condition class, and the efficiency of storage products.

Entry into force (Article 10)

The regulation entered into force on the twentieth day after publication (7 April 2019) and applies directly in every Member State without national transposition.
