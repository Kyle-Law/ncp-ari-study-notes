Three questions define any architecture

The paper argues that you can describe any liquid cooling setup with three choices, made in order from the chip outward. The paper deliberately skips the first one and focuses on the last two.

  1. 1

    How is heat captured in the server?

    Cold plates carrying water or refrigerant, or servers immersed in a dielectric fluid.

    Outside the paper's scope
  2. 2

    What type of CDU?

    Defined by how it exchanges heat (to air or to water) and by its size and mounting (in the rack or on the floor).

  3. 3

    How does heat reach the outdoors?

    Through the existing air system, through the existing facility water, or through a new dedicated system.

What a CDU actually does

A coolant distribution unit sits between the IT loop and everything else. Its core job is isolation: the water in a chip's cold plate has much stricter requirements than facility water, which could corrode the tiny channels inside. Inside, a CDU is essentially pumps, a heat exchanger, filtration and controls, working together to deliver five functions.

Temperature control

Holds TCS supply temperature to what the IT vendor specifies, based on chip case limits.

Flow control

Pushes enough coolant through manifolds, connectors and cold plates in every rack it serves.

Pressure control

Manages maximum system pressure and differential pressure. Some units run under vacuum (a leak-prevention system) so a breach draws air in instead of spilling coolant.

Fluid treatment

Filtration and chemistry, often propylene glycol mixes, to stop biological growth and keep water clean.

Heat exchange and isolation

Moves heat out of the TCS while keeping its fluid physically separate from the rest of the plant.

Two attributes pick the CDU

Only two CDU attributes are "critical", meaning a wrong choice forces you to redo the architecture: the type of heat exchange, and the capacity and form factor. Everything else (controller, filter type and so on) can wait for detailed design.

Step 1: choose the heat rejection method

This choice fixes the heat exchange type. Rejecting into room air means L-A; anything involving water means L-L. The paper weighs four factors: compatibility with existing cooling, deployment size, speed of deployment, and energy efficiency. You rarely get all four; the most compatible option is usually not the most efficient, because air carries heat far less effectively than water.

Step 2: choose CDU form factor

Form factor is independent of Step 1, which is what makes the two-step approach work. The key trade is isolation versus scale: one CDU per rack contains failures, one CDU per row gets cheaper and more efficient as racks multiply.

Rack-mounted

Sits at the bottom of one rack; that rack gets its own TCS loop.

Advantages

  • Can ship pre-integrated and tested with the servers
  • A leak or contamination stays within one rack
  • Redundancy (1N or 2N pumps) set per rack
  • Simple for a traditional site adding a few liquid racks

Disadvantages

  • Cost per kW rises as rack count grows
  • Consumes IT rack space
  • Caps density near 40 kW (L-A) or 80 kW (L-L)
  • Commissioning a CDU per rack slows large rollouts
  • Many small pumps are less efficient than one large unit

When to use

  • Roughly 1 to 10 liquid-cooled racks
  • Speed matters and the deployment is small

Floor-mounted

Serves a row or several rows from one shared TCS loop.

Advantages

  • Lower cost per kW at scale
  • In retrofits, can drop into former CRAH positions to limit piping
  • Leaves rack space for IT
  • Supports higher rack densities
  • Faster install and better efficiency as rack count grows
  • Larger fluid volume gives more thermal ride-through

Disadvantages

  • Every rack on the loop shares common-cause failures (leaks, contamination, controls)
  • Takes up floor space

When to use

  • More than 10 liquid-cooled racks
  • Workloads such as training clusters can ride out losing flow to all racks on one CDU
  • No vertical space left in the racks

Glossary

TCS
Technology cooling system. The secondary loop running from the CDU through the IT equipment.
FWS
Facility water system. The primary loop between the CDU and the chiller or heat rejection plant.
CWS
Condenser water system. Carries heat from the chiller to a cooling tower.
CDU
Coolant distribution unit. Isolates the TCS and controls its temperature, flow, pressure and chemistry.
CRAC / CRAH
Computer room air conditioner / air handler. Conventional room air cooling.
L-A, L-L
Liquid-to-air and liquid-to-liquid heat exchange.
LPS
Leak-prevention system. A CDU that runs the TCS under negative pressure.
Ride-through
How long the fluid in a loop can absorb heat if cooling stops. Larger loops ride through longer.
