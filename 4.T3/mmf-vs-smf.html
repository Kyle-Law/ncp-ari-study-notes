Multimode vs. single-mode fiber

Both fibers have the same 125 µm glass outside. The real difference is the width of the core that carries the light, and almost every other difference (light source, reach, cost, colour) follows from that.

Exam objectiveDescribe the difference between MMF and SMF.

In one minute

  • SMF (OS2): ~9 µm core, one light path, lasers at 1310/1550 nm. Reach runs to kilometres. Pricier optics, but the cable survives several speed upgrades.
  • MMF (OM3/OM4/OM5): 50 µm core (legacy OM1: 62.5 µm), many paths, cheap 850 nm VCSELs. Reach is limited by modal dispersion to tens or hundreds of metres.
  • Jacket colour: yellow = single-mode, aqua = OM3/OM4, lime = OM5, orange = legacy OM1/OM2.
  • Optics names say the fiber: SR = multimode, DR / FR / LR / ER = single-mode. NVIDIA prefixes: MMA = multimode, MMS = single-mode transceiver.
  • Never mix them in one link: the cores don't line up, so most of the light is lost at the joint.

It starts with the core

Why a wide core limits distance

In a wide core, light takes many paths ("modes"). Steep paths cover more distance and arrive later, so each pulse smears out. Over a long link, neighbouring bits blur together and the receiver can't tell a 1 from a 0. This is modal dispersion, and single-mode fiber essentially doesn't have it.

Illustrative model, not a link budget. Real multimode fiber is graded-index, so rays curve gently rather than bouncing, but the effect (different paths, different arrival times) is the same.

Connector colour ≠ jacket colour

A green connector body means APC (angled polish), not OM5. NVIDIA's NDR passive fiber cables use MPO-12/APC ends. Read the jacket and the label, not just the plug.

Exam traps

  • "Multimode is cheaper, so it's cheaper overall."MMF optics are cheaper. MMF cable costs more per metre. For short links MMF usually wins on total cost, and beyond its reach SMF is the only option.
  • "Single-mode can't be used for short links."It can. DR optics (500 m) are often used for short AI-fabric links too, because the same cable plant carries future speeds.
  • Mixing fiber types.An SR (multimode) optic on single-mode cable, or a DR optic on multimode cable, won't work properly. Optic and fiber must match.
  • Colour confusion.Yellow jacket = SMF, aqua = OM3/OM4, lime = OM5. Green connector = APC polish.
  • Wavelengths.850 nm = multimode VCSEL. 1310 nm = single-mode data-center optics (1550 nm for long haul).

Check yourself

1. What is the core diameter of OM4 multimode fiber?

OM2 through OM5 all use a 50 µm core. 9 µm is single-mode, 62.5 µm is legacy OM1, and 125 µm is the cladding on every type.

2. What mainly limits multimode reach?

Many light paths arrive at slightly different times, smearing pulses. Higher lane rates make it worse.

3. A cable has a yellow jacket. What should you pair it with?

Yellow = single-mode, which pairs with DR/FR/LR optics. SR optics are multimode.

4. Which light source and wavelength is typical for multimode optics?

Cheap VCSELs at 850 nm are what keep multimode optics inexpensive. Single-mode uses DFB/EML lasers at 1310/1550 nm.

5. A 300 m link between two rows needs to run at 800G today and faster later. Best choice?

300 m is beyond multimode reach at 800G-class lane rates, and single-mode carries future speed upgrades by swapping only the optics.
