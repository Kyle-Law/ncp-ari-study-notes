Bundling and dressing cables professionally

Dressing is how cables are bundled, tied, supported, coloured and labelled after they're plugged in. Done well, any cable can be traced, any part swapped and air moves freely. Done badly, a 2-minute transceiver swap turns into an outage.

Exam objectiveBundle and dress cables professionally (hook and loop fasteners, soft ties, color coding).

In one minute

  • Hook-and-loop (velcro) or soft ties, never plastic zip ties on network cables. Zip ties get overtightened, crush fiber and copper pairs, and must be cut to make changes.
  • Ties every 30–60 cm (12–24 in), supports at least every 2 m (tighter in SuperPOD racks: ≤ 60 cm). Tension: snug, stops movement without deforming the jacket.
  • Colour code by purpose or endpoint (tie colour = link group), keep copper and fiber in separate runs, and label both ends (and along long runs) with unique identifiers, e.g. TIA-606-B style.
  • Don't over-bundle or stack bundles: cables at the bottom get crushed. Respect bend radius, don't twist near the connector (≥ 75 cm away), and keep each U's cables in their own horizontal plane.
  • Keep transceivers, fans, PSUs and vents unobstructed, and remove abandoned cables. They still block air.

Fasteners: what to use

✓ Hook-and-loop / soft ties

  • Wide, soft contact spreads pressure
  • Re-openable for moves, adds and changes
  • Hard to overtighten
  • Colours available for coding

✕ Plastic zip ties

  • Thin edge concentrates force and crushes fiber
  • Easy to overtighten; deforms copper pairs
  • Must be cut, which risks nicking jackets
  • Never on fiber; avoid on network cables entirely

Colour coding and labels

Colour tells you what kind of link it is at a glance. The label tells you exactly which link it is. You need both.

Colour

  • Tie colour = link group, e.g. compute fabric, storage fabric, in-band management, OOB management, uplinks. Document the scheme and use it site-wide.
  • Fiber jacket colour already means something (yellow SMF, aqua OM3/OM4, lime OM5), so code with ties, labels or boots, not by inventing new jacket meanings.
  • Group bundles by purpose, e.g. inter-switch links in one, uplinks to core in another.

Labels

  • Label both ends, and along long runs, with machine-printed labels.
  • Each label names its own end first, then the far end. TIA-606-B form: floor+room.grid-RU:port, e.g. 1A.AD02-40:02.
  • Bundles carry a bundle serial number (BSN) matching the deployment plan.
  • Label so it reads without twisting the cable or blocking the port LED.

Dressing rules that protect the cable

Bend radius

Follow the datasheet. Rule of thumb ~10× OD static, ~20× while pulling. Tight bends crack fiber or deform copper pairs, and the damage is often invisible.

No twist at the plug

If a connector must rotate to fit, start the twist at least 75 cm down the cable so the connector isn't stressed.

Don't over-bundle

Huge bundles and stacked bundles compress the cables at the bottom and can degrade them. Split into manageable groups.

Weight off the port

Tie cables to the rack structure. No more than 2 m unsupported. Use trays and managers for continuous support.

Clear paths

Nothing in front of transceiver pull-paths, fans, PSUs or vents. Don't drape over other equipment or thread through holes.

Remove dead cable

Abandoned cables carry no traffic but still block airflow and fill trays. Removing them is a cooling fix.

Exam traps

  • "Zip ties are fine if not too tight."NVIDIA's guidance is soft hook-and-loop. Plastic ties crush cable and must be cut off.
  • Tighter is tidier.Snug is the target: stops movement without deforming the jacket. Tight wraps damage cables.
  • Colour without labels.Colour shows the group, not the link. Every cable still needs a unique label at both ends.
  • Mixing copper and fiber in one bundle.Keep separate runs: weight, crush and EMI differ.

Check yourself

1. Which fastener should bundle fiber in an NVIDIA cluster?

Soft hook-and-loop spreads pressure and can be reopened. Zip ties crush fiber and must be cut.

2. Recommended spacing for hook-and-loop ties along a bundle?

NVIDIA's guidelines: velcro every 30–60 cm, supports at least every 2 m.

3. What does colour coding give you that a label doesn't?

Colour shows the category. The label identifies the specific link and both endpoints.

4. Why remove abandoned cables?

Dead cables obstruct air and can contribute to overheating, and they waste tray capacity.
