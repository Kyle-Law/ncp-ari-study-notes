Wyr-Grid overhead cable tray

A plain-language walkthrough of Panduit's two-page specification sheet: what the system is, how the parts fit together, how many cables each size holds, and how to read the part numbers.

Source: Panduit spec sheet FRSP13--WW-ENG, dated 8/2019 · original PDF

What it is

Wyr-Grid is a wire-mesh cable tray that hangs above equipment racks — typically from the ceiling, a wall, or the tops of cabinets — and carries copper data cable, fiber, or power cable from place to place. The sheet positions it for data centers, connected buildings, and industrial automation sites.

Think of it as a modular steel "shelf" made of 10-foot sections. You join sections end to end with splices, add low walls only where you need them, add curved guides where cables turn or drop down to equipment, and hang the whole thing on brackets. It can also sit alongside Panduit's FiberRunner (a separate plastic duct system for fiber), so copper and fiber run in separate pathways.

    Why it's designed this way

    The sheet's list of "key features" is mostly about one thing: cutting installation time. Here is each one, translated.

    Better strength-to-weight
    Panduit says the sections are stronger for their weight than competing wire basket, so they are lighter to lift and quicker to hang.
    Large 4" × 5" grid openings
    Cables — and even Panduit's QuickNet pre-terminated cassettes — can pass straight through the floor of the tray without cutting wires out.
    No built-in sidewalls
    Ordinary wire basket has walls on both sides, which must be cut at every tee, cross, and drop. Wyr-Grid is a flat mesh, so there is far less cutting.
    Snap-on sidewalls
    Walls of 2", 4", or 6" are clipped on by hand only where cables need to be held in.
    Bend-radius control at intersections
    Curved wall pieces at tees and crossings stop cables from kinking around a sharp corner. They give a 3" (76 mm) bend radius and fit without fabrication.
    Bonding splice
    The splice's thread-cutting screw bites through the paint on both sections, so the run is electrically continuous end to end. That's what lets the tray serve as an equipment grounding conductor under the NEC.
    Snap-on waterfalls
    Curved guides where cables leave the tray and drop to equipment, again with a 3" bend radius and no cutting.

    What the numbers assume

    The sheet doesn't state its fill rule, but the arithmetic reveals it: in every row, the cables' combined cross-sectional area is about 50% of the internal area. For example, 115 Cat 6A cables × 0.0707 in² each ≈ 8.1 in², which is half of the 8" × 2" tray's 16.3 in². A 50% fill is a common design ceiling that leaves room for air gaps between round cables and for future additions.

    One correction

    In the printed table, the 8" wide × 6" sidewall row lists 537 under Cat 6A and 344 under Cat 6 — the reverse of every other row. Scaling from the 2" and 4" rows shows the columns are swapped, so this page uses 344 Cat 6A and 537 Cat 6. Also note "X" is the actual inside width (e.g. 24.2" for the 24" tray) and "Y" is the sidewall height.

    Reading a part number

    Most parts follow the same pattern: a WG prefix, a code for the part, a size, and a two-letter finish. In the sheet, ** means "insert the finish code here".

    PartCategoryPart number

    "BL / EZ" means the part is offered in both black powder coat and electro-zinc. Tray liners and divider walls come only in pre-galvanized (PG). The 24" FiberRunner trapeze bracket is printed as WG24RFTBBL, which looks like a typo for WG24FRTBBL — confirm with Panduit before ordering.

    Standards it meets

    • UL Classified as an equipment grounding conductor under NEC sections 392.3(C) and 392.7(B) — the tray itself can carry fault current to ground.
    • CAN/CSA-C22.2 No.126.1-02 — Canadian metal cable tray standard.
    • NEMA VE 1-2002 and NFPA 70 (NEC) — US cable tray and electrical code requirements.
    • ASTM A653 / A641 — corrosion resistance of the galvanized steel and wire.
    • EN 61537:2007 — European cable tray and ladder standard.
