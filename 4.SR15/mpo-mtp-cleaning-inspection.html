Cleaning and inspecting MPO/MTP connectors

One connector, a whole row of fibers. A plain-language walkthrough of Fluke Networks' guidance: what these connectors are, how to clean them, and how to prove they're clean.

Based on the Fluke Networks knowledge base article for MultiFiber Pro (published 14 May 2019).

What the article is about

MPO/MTP connectors terminate multi-fiber trunk links of 12, 10, or 8 fibers. Instead of one fiber per connector like LC or SC, all the fibers sit side by side in a single ferrule. That's great for density, but it means a speck of dust on any one of them can degrade a channel, and ordinary single-fiber cleaning sticks and microscopes can't reach them properly.

So the article's core point is simple: specialized equipment is required for MPO endface cleaning and inspection, and the process always runs in a loop.

Cleaning: the tool

Fluke Networks recommends a mechanical cleaner, specifically their QuickClean MPO Cleaner. A mechanical cleaner pushes a fresh strip of cleaning tape across the whole ferrule face each time you click it, so all the fibers get wiped in one motion.

It works on both UPC and APC MPO/MTP connectors, and on both loose cable connectors and bulkhead ports (the sockets in a panel or adapter).

Caution from the article
Read and follow the instructions for whatever cleaning device you use. Misusing it can damage the tool or the connector, or push contamination onto the endfaces instead of removing it.

Cleaning: the three steps

1

Prepare the cap

What you remove depends on what you're cleaning.

For a bulkhead port, take the guide cap off completely so the cleaner tip fits into the port.

2

Push until it clicks

Push the cleaner straight into the connector until you hear a loud click, then pull it out. The click is the cleaning stroke.

3

Inspect it

Look at the endfaces with a fiber microscope that has an MPO probe tip, such as the FI-1000 FiberInspector. Still dirty? Clean and inspect again.

Inspection: the setup

Inspection is what proves the cleaning worked. The FI-1000 FiberInspector plugs into the Versiv tester over USB and runs through the FiberInspector app in the Tools menu. You then fit a probe tip that matches the connector.

One detail is easy to miss: the MPO probe tips are shaped to go into a bulkhead port. To look at the end of a loose cable connector, you first attach an MPO-to-MPO adapter so the plug becomes a port the tip can enter.

Choosing a probe tip

Two rules from the article. If you inspect many MPO/MTP connections, pick tips with X (horizontal) and Y (vertical) adjustment, because the microscope can only show part of the fiber row at once and you need to pan across it. And if your connectors are APC, you must use an APC probe tip.

Try it: inspect an MPO endface

Two things from the article are easiest to understand by doing them. First, only manual grading is possible on MPO images, so you are the judge. Second, the scope only shows a few fibers at a time, so you use the X knob to pan along the row.

Pan across all 12 endfaces, clean, and inspect again until everything passes. The round black button works like the one on the FI-1000 probe: it freezes and unfreezes the image.

Why cleaning resets your inspection
After every clean you have to look again. A cleaner can move debris from one fiber to its neighbour, so a fiber that passed before cleaning hasn't been proven clean after it.

Support's tip: use the probe button

The small black button on the bottom of the FI-1000 camera probe does more than it looks like. The first press launches FiberInspector on the Versiv. After that, each press toggles between a frozen screen capture and the live view. Save your images from the frozen view, since a still image is sharper than a moving one.

If the button doesn't behave this way, check that your Versiv software is up to date.

The 2019 update

In March 2019 Fluke Networks added the FI-3000 FiberInspector Pro, a camera built specifically for MPO inspection, with probe tips for both UPC and APC MPO/MTP endfaces. It is the purpose-built alternative to the FI-1000 plus MPO tips described above.

The whole article in four lines
Clean every MPO/MTP connector before connecting, using a mechanical MPO cleaner.
Always inspect after cleaning; repeat until every fiber is clean.
Use an MPO probe tip (APC tip for APC connectors, an adapter for cable plugs).
Grade the image yourself, panning across the whole row.
