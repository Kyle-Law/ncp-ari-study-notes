Documenting the cable deployment

The design says where every cable should go. The as-built records where it actually went, with proof. When a link flaps at 3 a.m. six months later, those records are what the on-call engineer has to work from.

Exam objectiveDocument cable deployment with photos and as-built diagrams.

In one minute

  • As-built = the design updated with every field change ("redlines"): what was really installed, where, with which part.
  • Deliver a set: cable schedule (every link: ID, both ends, part, length, test result), diagrams (rack elevations front/rear, port maps, pathway/tray routes, logical topology), photos, and test records (loss, OTDR, inspection images, link/CVT reports).
  • Photos: each rack front and rear after dressing, switch faceplates with legible labels, patch panels, tray routes and drops, grounding points, anything that deviates from the design. Take them before doors close, and name them consistently.
  • Use one identifier scheme everywhere (TIA-606): the label on the cable, the row in the schedule and the port on the drawing all match.
  • Records are living documents: update them on every move, add or change.

What goes in the package

Cable schedule

One row per cable: unique ID, A-end (room.rack-RU:port, device, port), B-end, part number and media, length, bundle serial number (BSN), polarity type, test result and date, installer.

Rack elevations

Front and rear of every rack: devices by RU, PDUs, cable managers, which side each bundle uses (50/50).

Port maps & topology

Switch faceplate maps (which cage → which node/spine) and the logical fabric (leaf/spine/rails) the CVT validates against.

Pathways

Overhead/underfloor tray routes, drops, fill levels, firestop penetrations, grounding/bonding points.

Test records

OLTS loss per fiber, OTDR traces where taken, end-face inspection images, link status / ibdiagnet / CVT output.

Photos

Proof of workmanship and a baseline for the future. See the shot list below.

Exam traps

  • Handing over the design as the as-built.The as-built must include every field change.
  • Photos that can't be read.Wide shots alone don't prove labeling. Take faceplate close-ups too.
  • Different IDs in different places.Label, schedule and drawing must use the same identifier.
  • Documentation "at the end".Capture redlines and photos as you go, before ceilings, doors and tiles close.

Check yourself

1. What makes a drawing an "as-built"?

As-builts are the design updated with redlines from the field.

2. A cable moved from port 7 to port 9 because cage 7 was damaged. What must be updated?

Every record that references that link must match reality.

3. Best practice for deployment photos?

Photos are proof of workmanship and the baseline for future changes.
