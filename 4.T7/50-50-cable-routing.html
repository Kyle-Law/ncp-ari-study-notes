The 50/50 left-right routing strategy

A 64-port NDR switch can have dozens of thick cables plugged into one U. NVIDIA's rule is simple: half go left, half go right, and nothing crosses the middle of the rack.

Exam objectiveImplement 50/50 left-right cable routing strategy.

In one minute

  • Cables bound for the same switch at the same U are split in half. The left half of the ports route to the left vertical cable manager, and the right half to the right.
  • Result: cable volume and weight are balanced across both sides, no cable crosses the rack's centre line, and any switch or line card can be slid out without unplugging its neighbours.
  • Never route through the middle of the rack: it blocks airflow and buries the equipment behind it.
  • Order matters too: bundles run to the switch nearest the cable entry first, but connectors are plugged bottom-up. Connect and dress one U at a time, one side at a time.

See the difference

Serviceability

With the centre clear, a switch, line card, fan or PSU can be pulled without disturbing working links next to it.

Airflow

Cables crossing the face block the intakes and vents that keep high-radix switches and GPU nodes out of thermal throttling.

Weight balance

Copper is ~0.045 kg/m. Splitting the load keeps either manager from bulging and stops connectors carrying the weight of the cable.

Order of work

  1. Run order follows the feedTop-fed rack: run bundles to the top switch first. Bottom-fed: bottom switch first. Always start with the switch nearest to where the cables enter.
  2. Plug order is bottom-upRegardless of feed, seat connectors starting at the lowest switch and work upward. Cables from higher switches hang down, so working bottom-up keeps the next ports clear.
  3. Pick one side and finish itConnect the left half of a U's ports, then dress them into the left manager. Then do the right. Never plug everything first and tidy later.
  4. Keep each U in its own planeDress cables in the same horizontal plane as the U they plug into, starting near the middle and guiding outward. Outer ports have shorter runs, so tuck the extra length into the in-rack manager.
  5. Strap loosely, then snugHook-and-loop wraps loose while working. Once the side is done, tighten to just snug (not deforming the jacket). Supports no more than 60 cm (2 ft) apart.

Exam traps

  • "Route everything to the side nearest the patch panel."That's the one-sided pattern 50/50 exists to prevent.
  • Run order vs plug order.A top-fed rack runs bundles to the top switch first, but connectors still go in bottom-up.
  • Plug all, dress later.Connecting and dressing are interleaved: one U, one side at a time.
  • Through the middle "just this once".One cable through the centre blocks a switch from being pulled and sits in the airflow path.

Check yourself

1. In the 50/50 strategy, where do the cables from a switch's left-half ports go?

Left ports → left manager, right ports → right manager, so no cable crosses the centre line.

2. What's the main service benefit of keeping the rack's centre clear?

With no cables across the face, modules and whole switches can be extracted without disturbing working links.

3. A rack is fed from overhead trays. In what order are connectors seated?

Bundles run to the top first (nearest the feed), but plugging is always bottom-up so finished cables hang below your working area.

4. How are connecting and dressing scheduled?

Plugging everything first creates tangles that get worse with every cable added.
