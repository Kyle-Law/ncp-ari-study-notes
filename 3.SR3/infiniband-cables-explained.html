What InfiniBand is for

InfiniBand (IB) is a networking fabric built for one job: moving data between computers with as little delay as possible. Ethernet has caught up on raw bandwidth, but for workloads where microseconds matter, such as supercomputing, AI training, high-frequency trading and large analytics clusters, the article explains that IB is still often the default choice.

Its main trick is RDMA (remote direct memory access): the network adapter reads and writes another machine's memory directly, so the data skips the operating system's networking stack and doesn't keep the CPU busy.

The article lists four design goals: very low latency through RDMA; the ability to grow modularly into huge fabrics of thousands of nodes; hardware support for collective operations (the "everyone share your results" steps that dominate AI and HPC jobs); and high link utilisation thanks to that lossless, credit-based flow control.

Speed generations: lanes × lane rate

InfiniBand speeds are quoted per lane, then multiplied by link width. Almost every deployment uses four lanes ("4x"), carried in a QSFP-family or OSFP plug. Pick a generation to see how its headline number is built.

Why HDR and NDR switched to PAM4

Up to EDR, every lane used NRZ signalling: two voltage levels, one bit per symbol. HDR and NDR use PAM4: four levels, two bits per symbol. That doubles the data carried without doubling the symbol rate, at the cost of smaller gaps between levels, which makes the signal more fragile and is one reason copper reach shrinks at higher speeds.

Connectors: what plugs into what

The single most practical point in the article: before ordering anything, check which cage your switch and your adapter actually have. A cable built for one shell will not fit the other.

The article's five-step checklist

  1. Match the generationHDR means QSFP56. NDR means OSFP on the switch and OSFP or QSFP112 on the adapter or DPU.
  2. Measure the distanceUp to about 2 m: DAC. 3–100 m: AOC. 100 m to 2 km: multimode or single-mode optics.
  3. Confirm the form factorOSFP or QSFP, and order exact vendor part numbers (for example NVIDIA LinkX parts) rather than "anything 400G".
  4. Check the environmentBend radius in crowded racks, shielding where there's electrical noise, and airflow in GPU-dense clusters.
  5. Balance budget and powerDAC is cheapest, AOC sits in the middle, optics cost the most but go furthest.

InfiniBand vs Ethernet

Both now ship at 200, 400 and 800G, so the article argues bandwidth isn't what separates them. The difference is in latency, determinism and how the fabric is managed.

InfiniBandEthernet (incl. RoCEv2)

Article's verdict: Ethernet for general-purpose networking; InfiniBand where latency, predictability and scaling of collective operations matter most. Worth noting that this is contested ground, and large AI operators run both successfully.

Where these cables end up

HPC clusters

The interconnect behind climate models, molecular dynamics and genomics, where both bandwidth and predictable latency are required.

AI training and inference

GPU-to-GPU and GPU-to-storage fabrics inside training pods. Hardware collectives speed up deep-learning frameworks, and 400G NDR cabling is quickly becoming standard in AI "superpods".

Data centers

Storage back-ends such as NVMe over Fabrics, low-latency east-west traffic, and redundant fabrics for resilience.

FAQ, paraphrased

What does "4X" mean?

Four lanes in one link. The actual payload rate then depends on the generation, encoding and implementation.

Is there a standard reach for each cable type?

No universal one. Reach depends on the exact part, generation, connector, copper gauge or fiber type, transceiver, temperature and the host's support list.

Which connectors do HDR and NDR use?

QSFP56 for HDR. NDR can use finned or flat-top OSFP, QSFP112, twin-port and breakout variants. A matching shell doesn't guarantee matching lanes, speed, cooling or compatibility.

Can one cable serve both InfiniBand and Ethernet?

Some specific NVIDIA cables and ports support both modes, others don't. You have to confirm the exact cable, switch, adapter, firmware and port mode.

Is active copper actually used?

Yes. Active and linear active copper exist for certain generations and lengths, but support is part-specific, so compare power, reach, latency, bend radius, airflow and cost.

Reading it critically

The article is published by a cable and optics reseller and closes with a sales pitch, so treat its reach and cost figures as rules of thumb rather than independent test results. It says as much itself: every figure depends on the exact part numbers, standards, cable construction and environment.

For real deployments, the authoritative references are the vendor's own validated-cable lists. The article points to NVIDIA's InfiniBand cabling overview and NDR cabling guide.
