ESD protection for sensitive components

You can't feel a static discharge below about 3,000 V, but some semiconductor devices can be damaged by far less. ESD damage is often latent: the part works today and fails weeks later. Protection means keeping you, the work surface and the part at the same potential, and draining charge slowly.

Exam objectiveImplement ESD protection for sensitive components.

In one minute

  • Ground yourself: a wrist strap (with its built-in ~1 MΩ resistor) clipped to bare chassis metal, an ESD jack or a grounded mat before touching components. Test straps regularly. Cisco specifies 1–10 MΩ.
  • Ground the work: ESD mat on a bonded bench, ESD flooring plus heel straps where used, equipment in grounded racks.
  • Keep parts protected: leave NICs, transceivers, AOC ends, DIMMs and drives in shielding bags / packaging until install. Handle by edges or the pull tab, never the contacts.
  • Environment: low humidity raises static dramatically. Keep RH in range (ASHRAE's recommended envelope; NVIDIA: unpack in the datasheet's storage humidity).
  • NVIDIA notes active cables and transceivers have ESD tolerance for normal data-center use, but standard ESD precautions still apply when unpacking and plugging in.

Handling procedure

  1. Prepare the areaGrounded mat or bench, wrist strap tested, humidity in range, no plastic, foam or paper clutter (charge generators) on the work surface.
  2. Connect yourself firstStrap snug on skin, clip to bare chassis metal, the ESD jack or the mat's ground point, before opening any bag.
  3. Unpack at the point of useKeep parts in shielding bags until installation. Lay them on the mat or the bag, never on bare bench or carpet.
  4. Handle correctlyCards by the edges or bracket, transceivers by the body/pull tab, never touching gold fingers, pins or the optical port.
  5. Install and storeSeat into the grounded chassis. Return spares to shielding bags, and cap optical ports.
  6. Maintain the programTest straps and mats on a schedule (e.g. daily strap checks), train staff, and audit. ANSI/ESD S20.20 is the common program standard.

Exam traps

  • "I didn't feel a shock, so no damage."Damage happens far below the ~3,000 V you can feel, and it's often latent.
  • Strap clipped to paint.Paint insulates. Clip to bare metal or the ESD jack.
  • Pink "anti-static" bags vs shielding bags.Metallised shielding bags protect contents from external fields. Plain anti-static bags only avoid generating charge.
  • Ignoring humidity.Dry air (winter, aggressive dehumidification) multiplies static. Keep RH in the recommended range.

Check yourself

1. Why does a wrist strap include a ~1 MΩ resistor?

It protects the wearer from fault current and bleeds charge gently. Cisco specifies straps at 1–10 MΩ.

2. How does low relative humidity affect ESD risk?

Walking on carpet: ~1,500 V at high RH vs ~35,000 V at low RH (classic ESDA figures).

3. Best place to keep a ConnectX NIC until installation?

Keep sensitive parts shielded until the point of use, and handle them while grounded.

4. Where should the wrist strap clip go?

It must reach ground through a conductive path. Paint insulates.
