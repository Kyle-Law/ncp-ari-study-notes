OSFP vs. QSFP: same lanes, different boxes

The form factor is the plug's shell: how many electrical lanes it carries, how much heat it can shed, and which cages it fits. Port speed is the number of lanes times the speed of each lane.

Exam objectiveDescribe the different transceiver form factors (OSFP vs. QSFP).

In one minute

  • The prefix tells you the lane count: SFP = 1, QSFP = 4 (quad), QSFP-DD = 8 (double density, a second row of contacts), OSFP = 8 (octal, in a bigger shell).
  • The suffix hints at the lane speed: QSFP28 ≈ 25G lanes (100G port), QSFP56 ≈ 50G (200G, HDR), QSFP112 ≈ 100G (400G, NDR).
  • OSFP is wider and taller (~22.6 × 13 mm face vs QSFP-DD's ~18.4 × 8.5 mm), so it has more room for heat. It suits high-power AI/HPC optics. QSFP-DD packs more ports per 1U and accepts older QSFP plugs.
  • OSFP and QSFP are separate families: no QSFP plug fits an OSFP cage, and NVIDIA offers no adapter between them.
  • NVIDIA InfiniBand: switches use twin-port OSFP (2 × 400G at NDR). ConnectX-7 comes in OSFP or QSFP112. QSFP-DD appears only on Ethernet switches.

The family tree

Every name builds on SFP (Small Form-factor Pluggable). The letter in front says how many lanes, and the number after hints at the per-lane signalling rate.

Port speed = lanes × lane rate

The form factor fixes the lane count. The modulation fixes the lane speed: NRZ sends 1 bit per symbol, PAM4 sends 2. Try combinations:

Exam traps

  • "OSFP is faster than QSFP-DD."Not inherently. Both carry 8 lanes. OSFP's advantage is thermal headroom, not lane count.
  • "QSFP-DD is used on ConnectX adapters."It isn't. QSFP-DD is a switch-only cage, and only for Ethernet. NVIDIA adapters and DPUs use OSFP, QSFP112 or QSFP56.
  • "A QSFP56 plug will work in an OSFP port with the right adapter."No NVIDIA adapter exists. Treat OSFP and QSFP as separate worlds.
  • Backward compatibility runs one way.A newer QSFP cage accepts an older QSFP plug, which runs on 4 lanes at its own speed. An 8-lane QSFP-DD plug won't run in a 4-lane QSFP cage.
  • Twin-port ≠ one 800G link.At NDR a twin-port OSFP carries two independent 400G links, usually split to two adapters.

Check yourself

1. How many electrical lanes do QSFP-DD and OSFP each carry?

Both are 8-lane. QSFP-DD adds a second row of 4 to the QSFP footprint. OSFP is an 8-lane ("octal") design in a larger shell.

2. A site has QSFP28 optics left over from a 100G network. Which new 400G switch cage can still use them?

QSFP-DD cages accept older QSFP plugs on their first row of contacts. OSFP has a different shape and won't accept them.

3. Why do AI/HPC clusters tend to choose OSFP?

OSFP's extra volume (and fins) gives thermal headroom for ~12–15 W+ modules. Lane count is the same as QSFP-DD.

4. ConnectX-7 adapters ship with which cage options?

ConnectX-7 comes in OSFP (single-port, flat-top plugs) and QSFP112 versions, so check the card before ordering cables.

5. What is the port speed of a 4-lane QSFP56 running 50G PAM4 lanes?

4 lanes × 50G = 200G, the HDR InfiniBand port speed.
