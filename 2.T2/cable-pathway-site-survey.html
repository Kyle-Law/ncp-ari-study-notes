Site survey for cable management infrastructure

An AI cluster puts hundreds of cables per scalable unit over everyone's heads. The survey checks, before installation, that the trays are big enough, the supports are strong enough, and there's room to install and maintain everything.

Exam objectiveConduct site surveys for cable management infrastructure (load capacity, clearances).

In one minute

  • Capacity: tray cross-section must hold the planned cables at ≤ 50% fill. N = (F/100 × A) ÷ (π × (D/2)²). Copper (~9.4 mm) takes ~10× the area of a 3 mm AOC.
  • Load: cable weight per metre (AOC ~0.009 kg/m, copper ~0.045 kg/m) × count, plus tray and hardware, against the support and anchor ratings. Supports typically every 5 ft (1.5 m) on straight runs and within 1 ft (0.3 m) of splices and transitions.
  • Clearances: NEC 2026 392.18(F) wants 12 in (305 mm) clear above trays to work on them (exceptions include IT rooms built to 645.5 and crossings). Also check sprinkler, light and duct conflicts, and headroom.
  • Entry points: top-of-rack penetrations and drops (waterfalls / bend-radius guides) sized for the bundles, rack-top clearance, and side-wall space in racks.
  • Note codes (NEC/IEC/BS 7671…), separation of power and data, firestops, and bonding of metal trays. Record it all with photos and measurements.

Survey checklist

  1. RoutesWalk every planned path from the cluster racks to the network/management racks. Measure lengths, elevation changes, crossings and conflicts with ducts, pipes, lights and sprinklers.
  2. TraysType (ladder, wire basket, solid bottom), width × depth, current fill (existing cables count), condition, bonding/grounding.
  3. SupportsHanger type and spacing (≤ 5 ft typical), extra supports at bends and within 1 ft of splices, anchors into the slab or structure, rated loads.
  4. Clearances12 in above trays (or a documented exception), headroom to work, sprinkler clearance, access along the run.
  5. Drops & entriesTop-of-rack openings and brush grommets, waterfalls or bend-radius guides, rack-top equipment conflicts, side-wall cable space.
  6. Separation & codesPower vs data separation, firestops at penetrations, local code (NEC / CEC / IEC / BS 7671 / NF C 15-100).
  7. RecordMeasurements, photos, issues and required remediations, fed back into the design package.

Exam traps

  • Sizing for today only.Count existing cables plus future SUs. A tray at 50% fill on day one has no room to grow.
  • Ignoring copper weight.Copper is ~5× heavier per metre than AOC. Long copper runs overload supports fast.
  • Duct parked on top of the tray.Running along the tray inside the 12 in space isn't allowed. A crossing is (exception 4).
  • Checking the tray but not the anchors.Supports and anchors are evaluated separately, each for cable + tray + structure.

Check yourself

1. How many 3 mm AOCs fit in an 11.5 in² tray at 50% fill?

0.5 × 11.5 ÷ (π × 0.059²) ≈ 524, NVIDIA's worked example for one SU (508 cables).

2. NEC 2026 392.18(F) clearance above a cable tray?

A minimum of 12 in, provided and maintained, with four exceptions (IT rooms per 645.5, industrial qualified persons, AHJ permission, crossings).

3. Typical support spacing for straight tray sections in the SuperPOD guide?

5 ft on straights; curves and longer spans need evaluation; splices and transitions need a support within 1 ft.

4. Why does NVIDIA cap tray fill at 50%?

Headroom lets new cables be pulled without damaging existing ones.
