Which cables will a ConnectX-7 card accept, and are they still sold?

This page is NVIDIA's qualified-parts list for the ConnectX-7 network adapter running firmware 28.48.1000. It lists roughly 385 NVIDIA cables and optical modules across ten speed classes, each tagged with where it is in its product life, followed by about 185 third-party parts that are known to work but come without a performance guarantee, and a list of tested switches.

The newest class (NDR / 400–800G) is almost entirely orderable; nearly everything at 56G and below is end of life. That single pattern is the most useful thing on the page if you are buying cables today.

A part can carry both an IB and an Eth rate (for example many HDR cables are also qualified at 200GbE). If the column says N/A, it was not validated for that protocol on this firmware.

Lifecycle: the column that decides whether you can buy it

The page defines seven lifecycle codes, which collapse into four practical states. Parts move through them left to right. Where a code is written like EOL [HVM], the bracket is the stage it was in before it went end of life.

What the speed names mean

InfiniBand generations have letter names; Ethernet uses plain numbers. Both are built from the same idea: several electrical lanes running in parallel inside one connector. A standard QSFP-style port has 4 lanes, so the port speed is the per-lane rate times four.

Copper or fiber: the reach decides

The descriptions mix five kinds of media. They differ mainly in how far the signal can go, which is why the same speed shows up as a 1 m copper cable and a 2 km transceiver.

Copper

Passive DAC (direct-attach copper, codes MCP…) is just wire with connectors: cheapest, lowest power, but only 0.5 to 3 m at NDR speeds. Active copper (MCA…) adds signal conditioning to stretch that to 4 to 6.5 m. The 30AWG / 26AWG notes are wire gauge: thicker 26AWG appears on longer runs.

Fiber

AOC (active optical cable, MFA…, MFS…) has optics sealed into both ends. Transceivers (MMA… multimode 850 nm, MMS… single-mode 1310 nm) plug into the port and take separate passive fiber patch cords (MFP7E…, MPO-12 APC). Single-mode goes much farther: 100 m, 500 m, 2 km (FR4), 10 km (LR4) and one 40 km ER module.

Splitters, and why the ends look different

Most of the NDR list is breakout cables. A switch port on NVIDIA's NDR switches is a twin-port OSFP carrying 8 lanes (2 × 400G). A splitter lets that one switch cage feed two 400G adapters, or four 200G adapters.

Letters after the dash usually encode speed, then length (001 = 1 m, 00A = 0.5 m, 01A = 1.5 m, 02A = 2.5 m) or, for modules, reach class.

InfiniBand or Ethernet: set it after upgrading

ConnectX-7 is a VPI card, so each port can run either protocol. The page warns that a firmware upgrade followed by a reset may change the default port type. You set it with NVIDIA's firmware tool:

Value 1 = InfiniBand and 2 = Ethernet is the standard mlxconfig mapping. Replace <mst device> with your device path (e.g. from mst status), and reboot or reset the card for it to take effect. Single-port cards only need LINK_TYPE_P1.






  
Fine print worth noticing

  
One transceiver is card-specific. The twin-port 800G multimode module MMA4Z00-NS-FLT is only supported on four ConnectX-7 models: MCX750500B-0D0K, MCX750500C-0D0K, MCX750500B-0D00 and MCX750500C-0D00.

  
EDR links use RS-FEC. The note under the EDR table says EDR links come up with Reed-Solomon forward error correction. If the other end is configured for no FEC or a different FEC, the link may not come up.

  
Some rows are listed twice. A few parts (for example MCP4Y10-N001-FTF and the six MFA7U10 HDR splitters) appear twice in their tables. The counts on this page include those repeats, so unique parts are slightly fewer.

  
The “HIBERN/ATE” tag on a few end-of-life rows marks parts that were parked before retirement; treat them the same as EOL.






  
Third-party cables and modules

  
The page also lists about 185 non-NVIDIA parts (Cisco, HPE, Finisar/Coherent, InnoLight, Eoptolink, Amphenol, Accelink, Juniper, Credo and others) that have been seen to work. The page is explicit that there is no performance guarantee, and problems with them go through a feature-request process rather than normal support.

  





  
Validated switches

  

    
The page title also promises a tested-switch list, which sits at the very end below the third-party table. The copy of the page used to build this explainer was cut off before that table, so its exact contents are not reproduced here.

    
In general, ConnectX-7 is paired with NVIDIA's Quantum-2 (NDR) and Quantum (HDR) InfiniBand switches and Spectrum-series Ethernet switches, but check the original page for the specific models and switch firmware versions validated with 28.48.1000.
