CDUs for liquid-cooled racks

A coolant distribution unit sits between the building's water and the chips. It keeps the two loops apart and delivers clean coolant at the right temperature, pressure and flow, so a 120 kW+ rack can shed heat that air alone can't carry.

Exam objectiveDescribe CDUs for liquid-cooled racks.

In one minute

  • Two loops that never mix: the facility water system (FWS) (primary) and the technology cooling system (TCS) (secondary, to the cold plates). A heat exchanger inside the CDU moves heat from TCS to FWS.
  • The CDU controls temperature, pressure and flow: pumps (typically N+1), a control valve on the facility side, filters, an expansion tank, sensors and leak detection.
  • Liquid-to-liquid (L2L) rejects heat to facility water (row/large CDUs, hundreds of kW to MW). Liquid-to-air (L2A) rejects to room air via fans (sidecars for a few racks, no facility water needed).
  • Form factors: in-rack CDU (one rack), sidecar (beside 1–2 racks), in-row / row CDU (a whole row via manifolds).
  • Liquid racks still dump some heat to air (e.g. ~17 kW per GB200 NVL72 rack), and pumps must be on UPS: lose flow and GPUs overheat in seconds.

How heat moves through a CDU

Heat exchanger

Plate HX that transfers heat between loops without mixing them. The approach temperature is how close the TCS supply gets to the facility water temperature (a few °C on good units).

Pumps

Drive the TCS loop. Typically N+1 (e.g. 2 duty + 1 standby) with variable speed to hold pressure/flow as load changes.

Control valve

Modulates facility water through the HX to hold the TCS supply temperature setpoint.

Filtration & expansion

Filters protect the fine channels in cold plates. An expansion tank absorbs volume changes and keeps loop pressure stable.

Sensors & controls

Temperature, pressure, flow and leak detection, with alarms and BMS/DCIM integration. A rising pressure drop often means clogging filters or cold plates.

How much flow does a rack need?

Heat carried = flow × specific heat × temperature rise. For water-like coolant, roughly 1.4 L/min per kW at a 10 °C rise. Try it:

Simplified: flow = Q ÷ (ρ × cp × ΔT). Real systems add margin for pressure drop, uneven distribution and one pump or CDU offline (N+1). Halving ΔT doubles the flow.

Exam traps

  • "The CDU cools the water."A CDU is a heat exchanger and pump station. The heat leaves through the facility plant (or room air for L2A).
  • Facility water to the cold plates.Never directly. Building water is variable and dirty; the TCS loop is clean and controlled.
  • Forgetting residual air heat.Liquid captures most but not all heat. Room air cooling must still handle the rest.
  • Pumps not on UPS.If the CDU pumps stop, chips overheat in seconds, faster than servers can shut down gracefully.
  • Ignoring the inlet limit.Facility supply + approach must stay below the vendor's coolant inlet limit (e.g. up to ~45 °C for GB200 NVL72) on the hottest day.

Check yourself

1. What is the CDU's heat exchanger for?

The HX keeps the clean IT loop isolated from building water while transferring heat between them.

2. An air-cooled hall with no facility water needs two liquid-cooled racks. Best fit?

L2A sidecars reject heat to room air for one or two racks, so no facility water is needed.

3. The three variables a CDU controls?

It delivers coolant at the desired temperature, pressure and flow to the cold plates.

4. Pressure drop across a rack has been slowly rising. Likely cause?

A gradual pressure rise usually indicates restriction: dirty filters or fouled cold plates.

5. Typical pump redundancy in a CDU?

A spare pump keeps flow if one fails. Sites also plan at least one spare CDU (N+1).
