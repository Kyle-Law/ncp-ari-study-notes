Balancing rack PDUs on AC racks

A three-phase rack PDU is really three circuits in one box. If most of the load lands on one phase, that phase's breaker trips while the other two still have capacity, and the uneven currents waste energy as heat.

Exam objectiveBalance the rPDUs for AC-powered racks.

In one minute

  • Three-phase power is three AC waves 120° apart (L1/L2/L3, or X/Y/Z). Equal currents cancel, so no neutral current flows and every conductor shares the work.
  • A 3φ rPDU splits outlets into branches (or alternates phase per outlet). Spread the load so phase currents are roughly equal. A common target is within about 10% of the average.
  • Imbalance means tripped breakers (one phase hits its limit first), extra heat and neutral current (in wye systems), and higher bills.
  • Stay under 80% of each breaker for continuous load (NEC). With A/B PDUs, check it after failover: one PDU then carries the whole rack, so each phase must stay under 80% with double the load.
  • Verify with the PDU's per-phase / per-branch metering. Alternating-phase outlets make balance close to automatic.

Why imbalance hurts

Tripped breakers

Each branch or phase has its own breaker. Overload one and that group of outlets goes dark, even though the other phases had room to spare.

Heat and waste

Current that doesn't cancel still flows through wires and connections and turns into heat, which the cooling system then has to remove.

Stranded capacity

The rack is "full" as soon as the busiest phase hits its limit. The headroom on the other two phases can't be used.

How to balance, step by step

  1. Know the PDUPhase type (1φ / 3φ delta / 3φ wye), voltage, input breaker, number of branches and their breaker ratings. Outlets are usually colour-coded by branch or phase.
  2. Plan from nameplate or measured drawUse real measured power where you have it (nameplate is a worst case). Group devices so each phase totals about the same amps.
  3. Mirror A and BPlug each dual-corded device's PSU 1 into PDU A and PSU 2 into PDU B, on the same phase position, so both PDUs balance identically.
  4. Check failover headroomEvery phase on each PDU must stay under 80% of its breaker with the whole rack on that one PDU.
  5. Measure and adjustRead per-phase and per-branch current on the intelligent PDU (web UI, SNMP, DCIM). Move a cord to the next outlet group if one phase runs high, and set warning/critical alarms.
  6. Re-check after changesEvery add, move or change can unbalance the rack. Alternating-phase PDUs let you change phase by moving a plug one outlet over.

Delta vs. wye in one picture

Delta (typical North America, 208 V)

Outlet groups sit between two hot wires: L1–L2, L2–L3, L3–L1, each at 208 V (= 120 V × √3). No neutral to the outlets. Balance the three branch pairs.

Wye / star (230/400 V regions)

Outlet groups sit between one hot wire and neutral at 230 V. 400 V line-to-line is too high for IT PSUs. Imbalance shows up as neutral current.

Exam traps

  • "Balanced means equal outlets per phase."No: balance current, not outlet count. One GPU server can draw more than ten switches.
  • Balancing only in normal operation.With A/B feeds, check each PDU with the whole rack's load on it.
  • Ignoring the 80% rule.Continuous loads (≥ 3 h) on standard breakers are limited to 80% of the rating.
  • Plugging bottom-up into the next free outlet.On a traditional branch-grouped PDU that fills one phase first. Plan placement, or use alternating-phase outlets.

Check yourself

1. A 3φ rPDU reads X = 14 A, Y = 6 A, Z = 7 A on 16 A breakers. What's the risk?

Each phase is limited separately. X exceeds 80% of 16 A while Y and Z have unused headroom.

2. In a balanced 3φ wye system, what flows in the neutral?

Equal currents 120° apart sum to zero. Imbalance produces neutral current.

3. Two PDUs (A and B) each read 7 A per phase on 16 A breakers. Is the rack safe for a single-feed failure?

Dual-corded load shifts entirely to the surviving PDU, roughly doubling each phase.

4. What's the easiest way to move a device to a different phase on an alternating-phase PDU?

Phase changes every outlet (X, Y, Z, X…), so the next outlet is a different phase.
