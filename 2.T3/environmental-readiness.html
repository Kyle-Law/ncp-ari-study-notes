Verifying environmental readiness

The hall has to be ready before the racks arrive: space to place and service them, power and cooling for their real load, and conditions inside the envelope the hardware is specified for. AI racks stress all of these at once.

Exam objectiveVerify environmental readiness (rack spacing, power, cooling, thermal zones).

In one minute

  • Rack spacing & access: aisles wide enough to install and service (TIA-942 guidance is roughly ≥ 1 m front / 0.6 m rear, more recommended), working space in front of electrical gear, and a delivery path (doors, lifts, ramps) that fits the rack and its weight.
  • Floor: rated load per rack position and along the path. A GB200 NVL72 rack is about 1,360 kg before coolant. DGX H100 is 130 kg per 8U system.
  • Power: A and B feeds installed, energised, labelled, and each able to carry the full rack under 80%.
  • Cooling: capacity for the heat load with N+1. Air inlet in the ASHRAE recommended envelope (18–27 °C). Liquid racks need CDU capacity, coolant temperatures and pumps on UPS.
  • Thermal zones: map where cooling is strongest (near CRAHs, contained aisles, liquid loops) and place the densest racks there. Measure inlet temperatures low, middle and high.

Rack readiness check

Enter a rack's plan and the site's numbers. The checks follow the formulas from the 1 · T1 guides.

Airflow ≈ 3.16 × watts ÷ ΔT(°F) CFM. Heat to air = IT load × (1 − liquid share). 1 kW ≈ 3,412 BTU/h. DGX H100: 10.2 kW max, 130 kg, 1,105 CFM at 80% fans (3 · SR2). GB200 NVL72: ~132 kW, ~1,360 kg, ~17 kW to air (1 · T1). Rack weight for 4 × DGX includes a ~150 kg rack and PDUs as an assumption.

The environmental envelope

Readiness walk-through

  1. SpaceRack positions match the floor plan; aisle widths and front/rear service clearance; overhead clearance for trays and drops; working space at PDUs and panels.
  2. Delivery pathDoor heights/widths, lifts, ramps and floor ratings along the route for the heaviest crated rack. OCP-style 48U racks (~600 × 1,068 mm) are not standard 19-inch footprints.
  3. PowerA/B whips or busway installed and tested, breakers and receptacles match the PDUs, circuits labelled, capacity per feed ≥ rack load ÷ 0.8.
  4. CoolingUnits commissioned with N+1; containment, blanking and floor sealing done; for liquid: facility water loop, CDUs, manifolds, leak detection, coolant within spec.
  5. Thermal zonesMap the heat load per zone against the cooling capacity serving it; put the densest racks where capacity and containment are strongest.
  6. ConditionsInlet temperature and humidity in range at low, middle and high positions; clean (no construction dust); fire suppression and monitoring live.

Exam traps

  • Total cooling ≠ cooling where it's needed.A room can have enough tons on paper and still hot-spot one zone. Check per zone and per rack inlet.
  • Forgetting liquid racks' air share.A liquid-cooled rack still puts some heat into the room (~17 kW for a GB200 NVL72).
  • Floor checked only at the rack position.The delivery path (lifts, ramps, raised-floor tiles) must carry the rack too.
  • Commissioning with dust.Construction dust in a new hall clogs filters and contaminates optics. Clean before the hardware arrives.

Check yourself

1. ASHRAE recommended inlet air range?

18–27 °C (64.4–80.6 °F) is the recommended envelope for all A classes.

2. Approximately how much airflow does a 10.2 kW server need at a 25 °F ΔT?

CFM ≈ 3.16 × 10,200 ÷ 25 ≈ 1,289. In line with DGX H100's ~1,105 CFM at 80% fans.

3. Before a GB200 NVL72 arrives, what floor check is easiest to miss?

At ~1,360 kg, the rack must be supported everywhere it travels.

4. What does "thermal zone" planning mean?

Cooling capacity is local. Densest racks go where capacity and containment are strongest.
