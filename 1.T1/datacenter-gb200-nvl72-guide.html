GB200 NVL72: one rack, 132 kW

NVIDIA's GB200 NVL72 puts 72 GPUs and 36 CPUs in a single rack that behaves like one giant computer. It takes every idea from the earlier guides to the extreme. Here's how its power and cooling work, and how to check a site can host it.

Inside the rack

The rack is sold and installed as one unit. Per NVIDIA's DGX guide it holds 18 one-unit compute trays, each with 2 Grace CPUs and 4 Blackwell GPUs, plus NVLink switch trays that tie all 72 GPUs together, power shelves, a bus bar, and liquid cooling manifolds. Click a part to highlight it.

Power path: 2N built into the rack

The GB200 has no normal server power supplies. Power whips from remote power panels feed the power shelves, which turn AC into about 50 V DC and send it up a copper bus bar to every tray. There are eight shelves in two halves, A and B. Each half alone can power the whole rack. That's the 2N idea from Part 1. Press the button to lose feed A.

What this means for the siteEach rack needs two feeds, and each must be able to carry about 132 kW alone. That's roughly 264 kW of connected capacity per rack.
Check the whole chainUPS, PDUs, remote power panels and breakers on both sides, just like Part 1, but sized for this load.
Don't forget cooling powerCDU pumps and fans need backed-up power too. If pumps stop, 115 kW of heat has nowhere to go.

Cooling loop: mostly liquid, some air

The GB200 NVL72 has no air-cooled version. Cold plates sit on the GPUs and CPUs, and trays connect to the rack's manifold with blind-mate quick disconnects: slide the tray in and the coolant connection clicks into place, drip-free.

Warm water helpsWith coolant up to 45 °C allowed, dry coolers or cooling towers can often do the job without chillers. In hot, humid climates, check how many hours a year that's actually true.
Air heat is not small17 kW of air heat per rack is more than a typical whole rack. Eight racks add about 136 kW, around 39 tons, to the room.
Flow balanceEvery tray must get its share of flow. Racks furthest from the CDU get the least pressure, so check them first.

Sanity-check any number you read

You'll find conflicting GB200 figures online. Some articles quote per-rack flows of 20 or 35 L/min, while vendor datasheets say about 130 L/min. The formula from Part 3 tells you which is realistic in seconds.

Site checklist for GB200

A walk from the delivery door to the roof. Every item is one of the three basic checks: find the limit, measure the load, keep a spare.

  1. Delivery path and floorAbout 1,360 kg per rack. Check the floor at every rack position, plus lifts, ramps and doorways the rack travels through.
  2. Two power feeds per rackEach able to carry the full planned load (about 132 kW) alone. Check UPS, PDUs, panels and breakers on both sides.
  3. Liquid capacity with a spareTotal CDU capacity with one CDU failed must exceed total liquid heat (about 115 kW per rack).
  4. Flow to every rackAbout 120–130 L/min per rack, including the furthest rack from the CDU.
  5. Water temperature on the hottest dayFacility water, plus the CDU's approach, must stay below the vendor's coolant inlet limit.
  6. Air cooling for leftoversAbout 17 kW per rack into the room, with N+1 cooling units.
  7. Pumps and controls on UPSIf CDU pumps lose power, GPUs overheat in seconds.
  8. Leaks and coolant qualityLeak sensors with automatic shutoff, coolant chemistry within the vendor's spec, and scheduled filter changes.
  9. Commissioning testsPressure test, flush and fill, flow balancing, load bank test, and failover tests of a feed, a pump and a whole CDU.
Plan for what comes next. NVIDIA's GB300 NVL72 reference architecture lists up to 142 kW per rack, with per-GPU power around 1,400 W. A site built with no headroom for GB200 may not fit its successor. Use the 142 kW option in the calculator to see how much margin you'd need.
