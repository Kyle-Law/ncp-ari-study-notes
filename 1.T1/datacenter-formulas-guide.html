Before you start: the units

Most confusion comes from units, not maths. Here's every unit used on this page.

W, kW, MWPower: how fast energy is used. 1 kW = 1,000 W; 1 MW = 1,000 kW.
kVA"Apparent" power the wires carry. Slightly more than kW in most systems.
PFPower factor, 0 to 1. The share of kVA that does useful work (kW).
VVolts: electrical "pressure". Common: 230 V single-phase, 400/415 V three-phase (208 V in North America).
AAmps: how much current flows. Breakers are rated in amps.
BTU/hrRate of heat, used mostly in the US. 1 kW = 3,412 BTU/hr.
Ton (cooling)12,000 BTU/hr = 3.517 kW of heat removal. Not a weight.
ΔT ("delta T")Temperature difference, such as how much air or water warms up.
CFM, m³/hAirflow volume: cubic feet per minute, or cubic meters per hour.
L/s, L/minLiquid flow. 1 L/s = 60 L/min.
kg/m²Floor loading: weight spread over an area.
%Load or margin as a share of capacity.

Putting it together

Every formula above feeds one idea. Once this clicks, you can check any resource in any data center.

Headroom = (capacity with one unit failed × safety margin) − measured peak load Positive: room to grow. Negative: already over the limit. The resource with the smallest headroom is the one you'll hit first.

Worked example: which runs out first?

Take the small room from Part 1: 40 kW of servers, one 100 kW UPS, and three 5-ton cooling units.

  1. Power headroom: 100 × 0.8 − 40 = 40 kW. (Single UPS, so no failure case; in real life you'd want redundancy.)
  2. Heat load: 40 × 1.05 = 42 kW ≈ 11.9 tons.
  3. Cooling with one unit failed: (3 − 1) × 5 = 10 tons = 35.2 kW.
  4. Cooling headroom: 35.2 − 42 = −6.8 kW. Already short.
  5. Answer: cooling runs out first. It's the thing to fix before adding any servers.
