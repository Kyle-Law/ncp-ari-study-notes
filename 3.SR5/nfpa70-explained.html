What the NEC is, in one paragraph

The NEC is the United States' model standard for safe electrical installation: wiring, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, equipment, and special locations like pools, hospitals and hazardous areas. NFPA publishes it, but it only becomes law when a state or city adopts it, often with local amendments. A new edition comes out every three years, and each one goes through the public process shown on this page.

    Who actually writes it

    Anyone can propose a change, but every proposal is acted on by a technical committee and has to survive several votes before it reaches print.

    How the code is built, and why 2029 will look different

    Since 1937 the NEC has used the same nine-chapter frame. The first four chapters apply everywhere; the next three add or modify rules for particular places, equipment and conditions. Chapter 8 (communications) used to stand apart from the rest, but the 2026 edition ended that independence.

    The 2026 edition added Informative Annex L as a preview map of the proposed 2029 layout, and already moved some material into place. For example, load calculations moved from Article 220 to new Article 120, and energy management moved from Article 750 to new Article 130.

    The plan is to apply the new structure after the 2029 First Draft is complete, so article numbers people have memorised for decades are expected to change.

    How to take part from here

    Participation is free with an NFPA account. Here's where each opportunity stands for the 2029 edition.

    1. Submit a public input Closed 9 Apr 2026

      Proposed new or revised text with a reason. This was the only stage where brand-new material could be introduced.

    2. Read the First Draft Report Expected 10 Jun 2027

      Shows each panel's action on every input, plus the "First Revisions" with new text underlined.

    3. Submit a public comment Closes 29 Jul 2027

      Respond to what's in the First Draft. Comments can't introduce unrelated new material.

    4. File a NITMAM Closes 21 Apr 2028

      A Notice of Intent to Make a Motion lets you raise an unresolved issue for a floor vote at the June 2028 Technical Meeting.

    5. Watch for issuance and local adoption About Sep 2028

      After the Standards Council issues the 2029 NEC, each state or city decides when, or whether, to adopt it.
