Installing overhead cable support infrastructure

The overhead system goes in before any cable: hangers, tray sections, splices, drops and bonding, all level and at the clearances the design calls for. Then the bundles are laid into it in a set order.

Exam objectiveInstall overhead cable support infrastructure.

In one minute

  • Lay out the route from the drawings (laser line), mark support points, check conflicts with ducts, lights, sprinklers and the 12 in access space above trays.
  • Hang: anchors into the structure, threaded rod and trapeze hangers (or rack-top/wall brackets), supports at ≤ 5 ft (1.5 m), extra at curves, within 1 ft of every splice and transition.
  • Assemble tray sections with bonding splices, add dividers, sidewalls, waterfalls and drop-outs at every rack entry, and level everything.
  • Bond and ground the tray system (TIA-607 / NEC). Label pathways.
  • Then cable: seat the first stretch, fish and tie the near end, lay the run, deal with slack, fish and tie the far end. Support bundles while working. Never exceed bend radius (20× OD while pulling).

Where do the supports go?

Set a run length and how many splices it has. The diagram places supports at the maximum spacing plus one beside each splice.

Spacing rules from NVIDIA's DGX SuperPOD guide (5 · SR1): straights typically every 5 ft, a support within 1 ft of splices and transitions. Manufacturer and engineer requirements govern.

Install sequence

  1. PrepareApproved drawings, permits, material check (sections, splices, hangers, anchors, waterfalls), lift/ladder plan, safety briefing.
  2. Lay outSnap or laser the centre line. Mark hanger points at the design spacing. Confirm clearances: 12 in above trays, sprinklers, lights, ducts. Avoid running alongside other services.
  3. Install hangersAnchors into structure (not into ceiling tiles or other trades' supports), threaded rod, trapeze or bracket, set to height.
  4. Place and splice trayLay sections, join with bonding splices, and support each splice within 1 ft. Add curves, tees and height transitions with their own supports.
  5. AccessoriesWaterfalls over every rack entry, dividers to separate media, sidewalls where cables need retaining, cable-entry brushes at rack tops.
  6. Bond, level, inspectBond to the grounding system, level the run, check fastener torque and clearances, label the pathway, photograph before cables go in.

Laying bundles into the trays

1 · Seat the first stretch

Lay about 8 ft of bundle in the tray at the start, plus the free length needed to reach the target U.

2 · Fish and tie the near end

Drop it into the rack and tie it off. Don't plug it in yet.

3 · Lay the run

Place the bundle into the tray end to end, supported with temporary wraps so it can't kink or stretch. Coil and unroll around obstructions.

4 · Deal with slack

Keep slack at the top-of-rack end, not at the congested core/spine end.

5 · Fish and tie the far end

Drop into the destination rack and tie off. Repeat for the next bundle.

Pulling rules

Pull by strength members or approved grips with a swivel, ≤ rated tension, 20× OD bend while pulling, roll off the reel (don't spin off the end).

Exam traps

  • Hanging from other trades' supports.Anchor to the structure with the tray's own rated supports.
  • Splice in mid-span with no support.A support goes within 1 ft of every splice.
  • No waterfalls.Cables dropping over the tray edge break bend radius.
  • Plugging while laying.Bundles are laid and tied off first. Connecting is a separate, later phase.

Check yourself

1. What goes in first?

The pathway is built and checked first, then cables are laid in.

2. Minimum bend radius while pulling, as a rule of thumb?

20× while under tension, 10× once installed (FOA guidance). Check the datasheet.

3. Where is bundle slack kept?

Core and aggregation switches already have huge cable convergence. Extra loops there choke the tray.
