Physical network design documentation

Before a single cable is pulled, the design package has to answer where every rack sits, which port connects to which, with what cable, along which path, and under what name. Installers build from it, and the as-built is checked against it.

Exam objectiveDescribe physical network design documentation.

In one minute

  • The package layers from logical to physical: topology → floor plan & rack elevations → port-to-port cable schedule → pathways (trays, drops, fill, load) → labeling scheme → bill of materials and bundle/deployment plan.
  • For NVIDIA DGX SuperPOD, the fabric grows in scalable units (SUs). One H100 SU is 32 DGX slots (31 nodes + UFM), 8 leaf switches, with roughly 508 cables per SU (252 node/UFM + 256 leaf–spine).
  • The cable schedule is the single source of truth: unique ID, A-end, B-end, part number, length, bundle (BSN), route. Labels and drawings use the same IDs (TIA-606).
  • Design docs also carry the physical constraints: rack power and cooling budgets, tray sizes at ≤ 50% fill, structural load, clearances, applicable codes (NEC, TIA, BICSI).
  • Topology files (e.g. for the Cable Validation Tool) let the as-built be validated automatically against the design.

Exam traps

  • "The topology diagram is the design."Logical topology alone doesn't say where cables physically run, how long they are, or what they weigh.
  • Planning trays at 100% fill.NVIDIA's guide says ≤ 50% fill, for airflow, bend radius and future adds.
  • Different names in different documents.One ID scheme across labels, schedule and drawings.
  • Forgetting codes and clearances.Pathways follow ANSI/TIA, BICSI and NEMA practice plus local electrical/fire codes.

Check yourself

1. Which document is the port-to-port source of truth for installers?

The cable schedule lists every link's ID, both ends, part, length, bundle and route.

2. Roughly how many cables does one DGX H100 SuperPOD scalable unit need?

252 node+UFM cables + 256 leaf–spine cables ≈ 508 per SU, per NVIDIA's guide.

3. Maximum tray fill ratio in NVIDIA's SuperPOD guidance?

Stay at or below 50% fill for airflow, bend radius and room for future cables.
