NEC 2026, Article 392 Cable Trays, Section 392.18(F)

Keep 12 inches clear above every cable tray.

The 2026 NEC replaces a vague "adequate access" rule with a measured minimum. Drag the obstruction below to see when an installation passes, fails, or leans on one of the four exceptions.

What changed from 2023 to 2026

Earlier editions asked for enough room to work on the cables but never said how much. That left the call to each installer and inspector, which led to uneven enforcement and occasional rework. The 2026 edition swaps the judgment call for a number.

2023 NEC "Adequate Access"

"Sufficient space shall be provided and maintained about cable trays"

The rest of the sentence asked for adequate access to install and maintain cables. No dimension, no exceptions.

2026 NEC "Access"

A minimum of 12 inches above the tray must be provided and kept clear for installing and maintaining cables, with four exceptions that allow less.

Also notice the wording shifts from space "about" the tray to space "above" it.

Why 12 inches

The reasoning behind the change pointed out that what feels like enough room to a tall, slim installer may not work for someone shorter or larger, so a personal judgment is a poor test. Twelve inches also matches the clearance used in ANSI/TIA/EIA-569-A, the telecommunications pathways and spaces standard, so power and communications trays now follow the same benchmark.

The four exceptions

Less than 12 inches is allowed in any of these situations. They are numbered here as they are in the Code.

No. 1 IT equipment rooms

Where the installation complies with 645.5 for an information technology equipment room. These rooms have controlled access and purpose-built layouts.

No. 2 Industrial establishments

Where maintenance and supervision conditions ensure that only qualified persons service the cable tray.

No. 3 Special permission

The authority having jurisdiction can grant written permission for a smaller space, typically where site constraints make 12 inches impractical.

No. 4 Equipment crossing at any angle

Equipment that crosses over the tray, at a right angle or any other angle, may pass through the 12-inch space. The short crossing leaves the rest of the run reachable.

What it means on the job

The rule says the space must be "provided and maintained," so it matters at every stage of a building's life, not only at rough-in.

Design and coordination

Reserve a 12-inch envelope above each tray in the coordination model so ducts, pipes and other trays are kept out, or routed to cross rather than run parallel.

Installation

Measure the space before the tray is loaded. Hanger drops are much easier to adjust than a finished run. If you need less than 12 inches, get the AHJ's special permission in writing first.

Later work

Other trades that add equipment later can't park it in the clearance space. Crossings are allowed under Exception No. 4; running along the top of the tray is not.
