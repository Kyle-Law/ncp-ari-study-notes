Two plugs for the same 800G job

Source: “Choosing Between OSFP and QSFP-DD: Key Considerations for 800G Optical Transceivers,” Approved Networks blog, Oct 28, 2025 — read the original. Note that it’s a vendor post: they sell both types.

What the article is about, in one paragraph

AI training, machine learning and HPC are pushing data centers from 400G to 800G links. Both QSFP-DD and OSFP can carry 400G and 800G, so the choice between them isn’t about raw speed. The article argues it comes down to four practical things: physical size and heat, compatibility with gear you already own, which workloads each suits, and above all what your switches accept.

Size, heat and power

This is the core trade-off. QSFP-DD stays close to the size of earlier QSFP modules, so a switch can fit more ports across its front panel. The article puts its typical draw at about 7–12 W, and notes that pushing toward the top of that range may need extra cooling.

OSFP is the bigger module, and the article frames that as its advantage: more surface area to shed heat, which lets it run at around 12–15 W or higher. That headroom matters for power-hungry optics in AI and HPC clusters.

Compatibility with older gear

The article gives QSFP-DD its clearest win here. Its cage accepts earlier QSFP generations such as QSFP+, QSFP28 and QSFP56, so you can keep using existing modules and cables while you upgrade in stages.

OSFP has a different shape, so older QSFP modules don’t fit without an adapter. The article describes OSFP as the better fit for greenfield builds, meaning new facilities designed from scratch around high-power equipment.

Where each one tends to show up

Both perform well at 400G and 800G. The difference, according to the article, is in who adopts them and why.

QSFP-DD

Cloud, telecom and enterprise data centers, where power efficiency, compact size and fitting existing infrastructure matter most. The article says many telecom providers favor it, and a broad range of 800G products use it.

OSFP

AI/ML clusters, HPC systems and research centers, where optics draw more power and thermal headroom is worth the bigger footprint.
