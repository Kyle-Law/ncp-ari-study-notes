Electrical safety: LOTO, PPE and 120 kW racks

An AI rack draws as much power as a small building. Before anyone touches it, every energy source has to be found, isolated, locked, drained and proven dead, by the people doing the work, with the right PPE for the hazard that's left.

Exam objectiveAdhere to electrical safety practices (LOTO, PPE, 120 kW+ environments).

In one minute

  • LOTO (lockout/tagout) in 8 steps: prepare → notify & shut down → isolate at the source → lock & tag every point → release stored energy → verify zero energy → maintain through handovers → restore safely.
  • One person, one lock, one key. Each worker locks every isolation point. Nobody removes someone else's lock.
  • Rules: OSHA 29 CFR 1910.147 (US), NFPA 70E Article 120 (electrically safe work condition), EU 2009/104/EC.
  • PPE follows NFPA 70E's arc-flash assessment: arc-rated clothing (PPE categories 1–4: 4 / 8 / 25 / 40 cal/cm²), voltage-rated gloves, face shield or hood, safety glasses. Use a tested meter to "test before touch".
  • 120 kW+ racks: multiple feeds (A and B, sometimes more), DC busbars carrying thousands of amps, power-shelf capacitors, battery backup units, and pressurised liquid loops. All of them are energy sources for LOTO.

Arc-flash boundary

Distance where incident energy falls to 1.2 cal/cm². Inside it, arc-rated PPE is required.

Shock boundaries

Limited approach (unqualified persons stay out unless escorted) and restricted approach (qualified persons with shock PPE and a plan).

Labels

Equipment labels show incident energy or PPE category and boundaries. Read them before opening anything.

Energised work is the exception

NFPA 70E's default is an electrically safe work condition (LOTO + verification). Working live needs justification, a permit and qualified people. Hot-swapping a PSU or transceiver as designed is normal; opening a power shelf or busbar area is not.

Exam traps

  • Locking out feed A only.2N racks stay live on feed B. Isolate every feed (and any BBU) to the rack.
  • Skipping verification."Test before touch": check the meter on a known source, test the equipment, re-check the meter.
  • Someone else's lock.Only the person who placed a lock removes it. Shift handovers add the new crew's locks before the old ones come off.
  • Forgetting stored energy.Capacitors, batteries, pressurised coolant and gravity (raised equipment) all survive the switch-off.
  • The tag alone.A tag warns. The lock prevents re-energising.

Check yourself

1. Which step comes right before starting work in LOTO?

After isolating, locking and releasing stored energy, you prove it's dead before touching anything.

2. A rack has A and B feeds. You lock out feed A's breaker. Is the rack safe to work on?

Redundant power means multiple sources. All must be isolated and verified.

3. Minimum arc rating for NFPA 70E PPE category 2?

Categories 1–4 are 4, 8, 25 and 40 cal/cm².

4. Which US regulation covers the control of hazardous energy?

OSHA 1910.147 is the lockout/tagout standard. NFPA 70E covers electrical safety work practices.
