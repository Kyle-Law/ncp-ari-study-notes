What the page is

It's a product catalog category page on Vertiv's website. Vertiv makes power and cooling equipment for data centers. This page sits under Thermal Management and lists the products that cool at the scale of a single server rack, rather than an entire room.

The page itself has very little text. Its core message is a short argument: faster networks and lower latency demand more computing, which packs more power into each rack, which generates more heat in a smaller space. Vertiv pitches its rack cooling as a way to keep that tightly packed equipment within safe temperatures while saving energy. The product list loads dynamically, and two helper tools sit at the top: a SwitchAir Finder and a SwitchAir Smart Selector.

Where rack cooling fits

Vertiv splits its thermal portfolio into categories by how and where heat is removed. Rack cooling is the most local of the air-based options.

  • Liquid cooling
  • Heat rejection
  • Outdoor packaged systems
  • Room cooling
  • In-row cooling
  • Rack cooling
  • Free cooling chillers
  • Evaporative free cooling
  • Thermal control and monitoring
  • Custom thermal

The closer the cooler is to the heat source, the less air it has to move and the higher density it can handle in a confined space. Rack cooling is also useful where there is no room-level cooling at all, such as a network closet or edge site.

The three product families

Each family solves a different problem. One manages airflow, one adds cooling to a rack that has none, and one cools a small cluster of high-density racks.

Fixes a specific airflow problem

Vertiv SwitchAir

Data center racks are designed for air that travels front to back. Many network switches, though, pull air in through their sides and are often mounted at the rear of the rack, right where the servers dump their hot exhaust. The switch ends up breathing hot air and can overheat.

SwitchAir is a duct that channels cool air from the front of the rack to the switch's air intake, wherever the switch is mounted. It doesn't make cold air itself; it routes the cold aisle air the switch is missing.

Adds cooling where none exists

Vertiv VRC and VRC-S

The VRC is a compact air-conditioning unit that mounts inside a standard 19-inch rack, like a server would. It's aimed at edge sites, branch offices, and network closets that have no proper data center cooling.

  • Up to 3.5 kW of cooling (about 12,000 BTU/hr), with variable-speed parts that scale output to the load.
  • Two versions: self-contained, or split with an outdoor condenser, to suit the building.
  • Works in enclosed racks, open racks and 2-post racks.
  • Monitor it on its own display or remotely over SNMP or Modbus with a plug-in card.
  • A built-in condensate pump removes the water it pulls from the air.

VRC-S bundles the VRC with an enclosed Vertiv rack and a Vertiv Geist switched rack PDU, creating a ready-made micro data center.

Self-contained keeps everything in the rack. Split moves heat rejection outdoors through refrigerant piping, useful when the room can't absorb the extra warmth.

VRC-S bundles at a glance

Cools a small cluster of dense racks

Liebert DCL

A modular cooling unit, roughly the shape of a rack, that attaches to the side of server cabinets. Hot air from the racks is drawn sideways into the DCL, cooled by water coils, and returned to the front of the servers in a closed loop.

  • Up to 35 kW of cooling per unit, ten times one VRC.
  • One unit can serve up to four racks, two on each side.
  • Designed for higher return-air temperatures, which raises usable capacity.
  • Liebert iCOM control with fail-safe logic: if the controller fails, fans and water flow go to maximum.
  • Monitors rack door status and can release the doors automatically; dual power supply and dual coil options.
  • Remote rack temperature sensors and monitoring over SNMP v3 or HTTPS.

Because the loop is closed inside the cabinets, the dense racks don't dump heat into the room, which makes the DCL useful for adding high-density racks to an existing space.

Capacity compared

The three families cover very different heat loads. SwitchAir has no cooling rating at all since it only moves air; the other two are measured in kilowatts of heat removed.
