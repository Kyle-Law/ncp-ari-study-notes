Rack cooling, explained

This page is Vertiv's product catalog for cooling that lives inside or on a single IT rack, rather than cooling a whole room or a whole row. It covers two families: the VRC rack-mounted air conditioner, and SwitchAir airflow guides for network switches.

What the page is actually saying

The catalog page's intro makes one argument: faster data transfer, lower latency and always-on availability push more computing into each rack, which raises power density per rack. Servers only run at full capacity inside the right temperature band, so the heat of tightly packed gear needs to be removed at the rack, efficiently.

Beyond that intro, the page is mostly a navigation shell. The product grid is loaded by script, and the sidebar points to two selector tools: the SwitchAir Finder (match by switch model) and the SwitchAir Smart Selector (match by specification).

Family 1: Vertiv VRC rack cooling unit

The VRC is a small, self-contained air conditioner that bolts into a standard 19-inch rack. It's aimed at small server rooms, network closets and edge sites, where there's no proper data-center cooling and the building's comfort AC isn't reliable enough for IT.

How it saves energy

Its fans and compressor are variable speed, so output tracks the real-time IT load instead of cycling fully on and off. It comes pre-charged with refrigerant and ships with a power cord and plug, so deployment is close to plug-in.

There's also a bundled option, the VRC-S micro data center (VR3300 42U rack with a 3.5 kW, 120 V cooling unit), filed under Integrated Solutions.

Family 2: SwitchAir airflow guides

Network switches, routers and load balancers are usually mounted at the top and back of the rack so their ports face the cabling. The catch: many switches breathe sideways or from the rear, so they end up inhaling the hot exhaust of the servers beside them. That drives up intake temperature and shortens their life.

SwitchAir is a rack-mounted duct that gives the switch a private path to cold air at the front of the cabinet, and blocks hot air from wrapping around to its intake.

Top-down view of a rack. Dashed lines are the front and rear mounting rails.

Passive

No power. Just a channel; the switch's own fans pull cold air through it. Most models are this type.

Active

Adds internal fans that push cold air from the front of the cabinet to the switch intake, for tougher placements.

Before you buy

  • Match the family to the problem. Whole rack too hot with no proper cooling nearby → VRC. Only the switches running hot in an otherwise cooled rack → SwitchAir.
  • Check your load. VRC tops out at 3.5 kW per cabinet. Denser racks belong to the In-Row, Room or Liquid Cooling pages.
  • Plan the heat exit. The self-contained VRC dumps compressor heat through a duct into a ceiling void, or straight into a large space like a warehouse. If neither works, ask about the split version.
  • Confirm current SKUs. At least one reseller lists the VRC101KIT (208 V) as end-of-life, so check what's currently orderable in your region.
  • Register SwitchAir. Standard warranty is 3 years, extended to 5 if registered within 120 days of purchase.
