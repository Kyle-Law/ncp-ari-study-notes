Designing cable support for ultra-high-density loads

Every scalable unit adds roughly 500 cables to the overhead paths, and they all converge on the management and spine racks. The support system has to carry that weight, keep cables within fill and bend limits, and leave room for the next SU.

Exam objectiveDesign cable support systems for ultra-high-density loads.

In one minute

  • Start from counts: cables per SU (~508 for DGX H100: 252 node/UFM + 256 leaf–spine), times SUs on each pathway, by cable type.
  • Area: tray cross-section at ≤ 50% fill. Weight: AOC ≈ 0.009 kg/m, copper ≈ 0.045 kg/m, plus tray and hardware, against the tray's rated working load and each support's and anchor's rating (checked separately).
  • Spacing: supports typically every 5 ft (1.5 m) on straights, extra on curves, and within 1 ft (0.3 m) of splices and transitions. Evaluate spans longer than 5 ft.
  • Converge carefully: density peaks at central management/network racks, so size those pathways and rack-top entries for the worst point, not the average.
  • Design for growth (future SUs), separate copper and fiber paths, bend-radius control at every drop, bonding, and code compliance (NEC/IEC, TIA, BICSI, seismic where required).

Design decisions

Tray type

Wire basket / mesh (flexible, easy drops), ladder (heavy loads, long spans), solid-bottom (protection). Choose by load, span and access.

Tiers & separation

Separate fiber and copper (and power) paths. Stack tiers with working clearance, and keep 12 in above trays for access (NEC 392.18(F)).

Supports & anchors

Trapeze hangers on threaded rod, wall/rack-top brackets. Supports and anchors each rated for cable + tray + structure. Add seismic bracing where required.

Convergence points

Size the paths above management and spine racks for all SUs that land there, plus rack-top penetrations and waterfalls.

Growth

Plan for future SUs: fill at day one well under 50%, spare pathways, room to pull new cables without disturbing the old.

Media choice

Fiber/AOC for long runs. Copper is ~10× the area and ~5× the weight, so keep it to short in-rack links.

Exam traps

  • Sizing for the average run.The heaviest point is where SUs converge. Design there.
  • Checking the tray but not anchors.Supports and anchors are evaluated separately.
  • Ignoring splices.Splices and transitions need a support within 1 ft.
  • Copper in long overhead runs.Its weight and bulk overwhelm trays. Use fiber.

Check yourself

1. Cable load per metre for one SU of 3 mm AOCs (≈ 508 cables)?

508 × 0.009 kg/m ≈ 4.57 kg/m from cables alone, NVIDIA's reference figure.

2. Where must a support be placed relative to a tray splice?

Splices, joints and height transitions need a support within 1 ft.

3. Where is cable density highest in a SuperPOD hall?

Every leaf–spine cable lands at the core. Size those pathways for the worst case.
