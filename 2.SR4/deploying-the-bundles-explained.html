Deploying the bundles

How pre-labelled cable bundles get from the staging area into the racks, in what order, and how they are plugged in and dressed so the rack stays cool and serviceable.

Every bundle enters a network rack one of three ways. The feed direction decides which switch gets its bundle first: you always start with the switch nearest to where the cables come in. Pick a feed to see the run order on the rack.

The whole job, in three phases

By the time this page applies, the room has been assessed, the cable trays surveyed, and every cable labelled and bundled. What is left happens in this order.

1

Inter-rack bundles

Long bundles that run through overhead or underfloor trays between racks. Laid in place, ends tied off, not plugged in.

2

Intra-rack cables

Short cables inside a rack, usually not pre-bundled. Plugged at one end and routed to the other.

3

Connect and dress

Seat connectors one U at a time and shape the cables as you go. Never as two separate passes.

The bundle serial numbers (BSN) usually reflect deployment order, but not always. Check the cable deployment documentation for the actual sequence before you start.

Plug in from the bottom up

Whichever way the cables enter the rack, connectors are seated starting with the lowest switch and working upward.

The reason is simple access. Cables already plugged into a higher switch hang down in front of the ports below, so every bundle after the first has to be fed in behind them. Starting low keeps the working area clear, since the finished cables are always beneath your hands rather than in front of them.

Run order (previous section) and plug order are separate things. A top-fed rack runs bundles to the top switch first, yet the connectors still go in from the bottom switch upward.

Split every switch's cables 50/50

Cables bound for the same switch at the same U are divided in half. One half travels up or down the left vertical cable manager and lands on the left half of the ports; the other half does the same on the right.

This balances the sheer volume of InfiniBand cable across both sides of the rack, and it means no cable ever crosses the rack's centre line. With the middle kept clear, any switch or line card can be slid in or out without unplugging its neighbours.

Never route cables through the middle of the rack. It blocks airflow and makes the equipment behind the cables impossible to service without disconnecting them.

Installing the inter-rack bundles

Move the prepared bundles from staging to the space right in front of the core switch or network rack. Then lay each one into the trays, end to end. Neither end is plugged in during this phase. Select a step to see where it happens.

Support the bundle while you work. Temporary wraps, straps, or any other support that stops it kinking or stretching. Where an obstruction stops you dropping it straight into the tray, coil the whole bundle and unroll it into place.
Keep slack at the top-of-rack end. Core and aggregation switches already have huge numbers of cables converging on them; extra loops there choke the tray.

Installing the intra-rack cables

Inside a rack, cables are normally not pre-bundled, and rack cable managers vary so much that the installer decides whether to start at the switch or at the node.

Plug the connector into its source port, then fish the cable to its destination port. Contain any slack in the rack's cable management, keeping within the bend-radius limits, and repeat for the rest.

Three things matter throughout: never exceed the specified bend radius, support the cables properly so they last, and once a cable leaves its bundle, give it a path that keeps other equipment, airflow and service access clear.

Connecting and dressing, together

Plugging everything in first and tidying afterwards produces tangles that get worse with every cable added. The guide treats connecting and dressing as one interleaved task: connect a U, dress it, move up.

Connecting

  1. Pick one side of the rack, left or right, and finish it first. Strip off any plastic wrap left from installation and any protective caps still on the connectors.
  2. For cables entering from the top, start with the bundles whose destinations are at the top.
  3. Read the label, then fully seat each connector in its port. You may need to unplug and re-plug a few neighbours to undo tangles, or twists from cables trying to spring back to their coiled shape. Cables to the outermost ports are shorter runs than those to the middle ports, so tuck the difference into the in-rack manager.
  4. Work one U at a time, alternating between connecting ports and dressing cables.

Dressing

  1. With one side of a U connected, start near the middle and gently guide the cables into shape. Respect bend radius, never pull, and keep the cables in the same horizontal plane as the U they plug into.
  2. Wrap with hook-and-loop at intermediate points and inside the cable manager. Keep these early wraps loose, since they will likely need adjusting.
  3. At the last cable, add extra wraps wherever there is slack, a loop or a bend.
  4. When everything looks right, tighten each wrap to just snug and close up the cable management.
