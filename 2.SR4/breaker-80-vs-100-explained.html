80% rated vs 100% rated circuit breakers

An illustrated explanation of Schneider Electric FAQ FA104355  |  read the original

Both versions of a breaker carry the same amp rating and trip the same way. The only difference is how much load they're allowed to carry for 3 hours or more: a standard breaker may carry 80% of its setting, a 100% rated breaker may carry all of it.

Try it: is this load allowed?

Same breaker, same trip curve

An 80% and a 100% version of a given breaker use the identical trip curve, so they react to an overload or a short circuit in exactly the same way. "100% rated" is not a stronger or slower breaker. It's a rating about how the breaker may be applied to long-running loads.

What "continuous" means: 3 hours or more

The National Electrical Code (NEC) calls a load continuous when its maximum current is expected to last 3 hours or longer. The 80% restriction only applies to continuous loads. Below 3 hours, a standard breaker can be loaded to 100% of its setting.

The 80% applies to the setting, not the frame

Many molded-case breakers have an adjustable long-time pickup, Ir. The FAQ's example: a 150 A PowerPact H-frame dialed down to 100 A has a continuous limit of 80% of 100 A, not of 150 A. Turn the dial to see the limit follow.

Why it matters: breaker size for the same load

In practice, the 80% rule means a standard breaker has to be oversized for a continuous load (by 125%, the inverse of 80%), while a 100% rated breaker can be matched to the load. That can mean a smaller breaker, and sometimes smaller equipment upstream. Pick a continuous load:
