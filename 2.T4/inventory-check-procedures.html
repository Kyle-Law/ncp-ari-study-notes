Inventory check procedures

A short count or one wrong cable type found mid-install stalls a whole row of racks. Inventory checks catch missing, damaged and wrong parts at the dock, while there's still time to reorder.

Exam objectiveDescribe inventory check procedures.

In one minute

  • Receive: inspect packaging for damage and shock/tilt indicators before signing. Note any damage on the delivery receipt and photograph it.
  • Reconcile: count every line against the packing list, purchase order and BOM, matching part number (not just description), quantity, length and variant (e.g. finned vs flat top).
  • Record: serial numbers and asset tags into the inventory / DCIM system, and map them to rack locations.
  • Store & stage: clean, dry, secure, within the datasheet's storage temperature and humidity, ESD-safe. Let equipment acclimatise before unpacking. Kit by rack or bundle (BSN) in deployment order.
  • Report shortages, damage and wrong parts immediately. Test suspect fiber reels for continuity on arrival.

The receiving flow

1 · InspectCrates, pallets, shock and tilt indicators, wet or crushed boxes
2 · CountEvery line vs packing list and PO; part numbers and quantities
3 · VerifyVariant details: lengths, top style, reach class, fiber type, polarity
4 · RecordSerials and asset tags into DCIM / inventory; photos of damage
5 · ReportDiscrepancies to the vendor and PM the same day
6 · StageKit by rack / bundle (BSN) in deployment order

Storage and staging rules

Environment

Clean and dry, within each datasheet's storage temperature and humidity range, away from construction dust. Open packaging in a space that matches storage humidity.

Acclimatise

Equipment moved from a cold or hot truck needs time to reach room temperature before unpacking and power-on, to avoid condensation.

ESD

Transceivers, AOCs and NICs are ESD-sensitive. Use grounded work surfaces and wrist straps when unpacking.

Protect the optics

Leave dust caps on. Never stack heavy boxes on cable reels or coils.

Reels

Lift by a pipe through the hub, never by slings around the cable. Keep both ends reachable for testing.

Kitting

Group cables and optics by rack and bundle serial number so installers pull exactly what the deployment plan calls for.

Exam traps

  • Counting boxes, not parts.Check part numbers and variants: a 3 m cable in a box labelled 5 m, or flat-top optics instead of finned, still matches the box count.
  • Signing for damaged freight "clean".Note damage on the delivery receipt with photos before signing.
  • Unpacking cold equipment immediately.Let it acclimatise to avoid condensation.
  • Reporting later.Shortages and wrong parts should be reported the same day, since lead times on AI networking parts can be long.

Check yourself

1. What do you reconcile received items against?

Part numbers and quantities must match what was ordered and what the design needs.

2. A pallet's tilt indicator has tripped. What now?

Document possible transit damage before signing, then inspect.

3. Why record serial numbers at receiving?

Serials tie each part to its rack position, warranty and support cases.
