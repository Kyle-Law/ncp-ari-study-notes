Two ways to feed a rack

Every server, switch and storage array runs on low-voltage DC inside. The real difference between an AC rack and a DC rack is where grid power gets turned into that DC, and how many times it's converted on the way.

Alternating current
Voltage swings positive and negative, crossing zero 100–120 times a second
Direct current
Voltage holds steady at one polarity, like a battery

Where the conversion happens

Follow the power from the utility feed to the chips. In an AC rack, power is converted back and forth several times, and the last conversion happens inside every single device. In a DC rack, it's converted once, centrally, and delivered as DC straight to the equipment.

What the rack looks like

The architectural difference is visible in the hardware. AC racks have power strips down the sides and a cord into every device. DC racks move the power supplies out of the servers into a shared shelf, and a copper busbar runs up the back.

Why voltage matters: the current problem

Power equals voltage times current. At a low voltage like 48V, a high-power rack needs enormous current, which means thick copper, heavy busbars, and more resistive loss. That's why AI-era racks drawing hundreds of kilowatts are pushing DC up to 800V.

The flavors of DC

"DC rack" covers a wide range of voltages, each with a different history and purpose. The dashed line marks roughly where DC stops being touch-safe (extra-low voltage, about 60V).

The arc problem

When you open a switch under load, an arc can form across the gap. AC helps put it out for free: the voltage passes through zero every half-cycle, and the arc tends to die there. DC never crosses zero, so an arc can sustain itself. High-voltage DC needs breakers, connectors and procedures specifically rated for it.

AC racks are the flexible default: any equipment, familiar skills, standard parts. DC racks trade that flexibility for fewer conversions, simpler battery backup and higher density, which is why telecom has used them for decades and AI data centers are moving toward them now.
