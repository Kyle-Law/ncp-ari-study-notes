A team orientation plan for the cabling workflow

A scalable unit means hundreds of cables, several people, and work at height. Before the first bundle moves, everyone needs to know the plan, their role, the order of work, the safety rules, and what "done" looks like at each stage.

Exam objectiveDefine a team orientation plan for the cabling workflow.

In one minute

  • Orientation covers scope (racks, SUs, cable counts), documents (cable schedule, elevations, bundle/BSN deployment order, labeling scheme), roles, sequence, safety, quality gates and communication.
  • Workflow phases (DU-10438): stage & verify → inter-rack bundles laid in trays (not plugged) → intra-rack cables → connect & dress per U, bottom-up, 50/50 → test (install one, test one) → inspect & document.
  • Typical roles: lead (plan, sequence, sign-off), pathway / pull crew (trays, lay bundles, slack), rack installers (connect, dress), test & QA (clean, inspect, verify links), documentation (as-built, photos).
  • Safety briefing: ladders/lifts and work at height, PPE, ESD, LOTO boundaries near power, laser safety with live optics, housekeeping.
  • Daily stand-up: today's BSNs and racks, blockers, findings from yesterday's QA, and any changes (redlines).

    Orientation briefing checklist

    Scope & documents

    • Racks, SUs and cable counts
    • Cable schedule, elevations, pathway drawings
    • BSN deployment order (check, don't assume)
    • Label scheme and examples

    Standards

    • DU-10438 rules (50/50, bottom-up, per-U dressing)
    • Bend radius, support spacing, twist ≥ 75 cm
    • Clean every insertion
    • Hook-and-loop only

    Safety

    • Ladders/lifts, work at height, overhead hazards
    • PPE, ESD straps, laser safety
    • No work inside LOTO boundaries
    • Housekeeping: caps, offcuts, packaging

    Quality & comms

    • Quality gates per phase
    • How to log a finding or a redline
    • Who to call for parts, power and cooling
    • Daily stand-up time and format

    Exam traps

    • Plug everything, dress later.The plan interleaves connect and dress per U.
    • Assuming BSN order = deployment order.Usually, but not always. Check the deployment documentation.
    • Testing at the end.Install one, test one. Finding a bad cable in a finished bundle costs far more.
    • No safety briefing.Work at height and around live power is part of every cabling job.

    Check yourself

    1. During the inter-rack bundle phase, are the bundle ends plugged in?

    Inter-rack bundles are laid end to end and tied off. Connecting and dressing happens per U afterwards.

    2. What should a daily stand-up cover?

    Short daily alignment keeps a multi-person crew on the same sequence and quality bar.

    3. Which role turns field redlines into the as-built package?

    Documentation keeps the cable schedule, drawings and photos matching what's installed.
