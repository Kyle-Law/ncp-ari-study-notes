Verifying grounding

When something inside a rack faults, current looks for the easiest way to earth. Grounding decides whether that path is a copper conductor or the person touching the rack.

Exam objectiveVerify grounding.

In one minute

  • Two separate jobs. Bonding ties every metal part together (racks, doors, rails, trays, ladder racks) so nothing sits at a different voltage. Grounding gives that bonded system one low-impedance path to earth.
  • The path is a hierarchy (TIA-607): rack → rack bonding conductor → SBB/TGB room busbar → TBB backbone → PBB/TMGB main busbar → building grounding electrode.
  • Each rack connects individually to the busbar or row conductor with a green or green/yellow conductor and listed compression lugs. No daisy-chaining, and bare metal or paint-piercing hardware (paint is an insulator).
  • The power cord's earth pin protects that device, not the rack frame, doors and trays.
  • Verify: visual inspection, continuity / bond resistance tests, ground resistance by the electrician, then recheck periodically and after moves/adds.

Good vs. bad installs

How to verify

  1. Check the requirementsEquipment manufacturer grounding instructions, TIA-607 / TIA-942 for telecom spaces, NEC (NFPA 70) or local code, and the site's uptime targets.
  2. Visual inspectionA ground busbar in the room tied back to the building ground. Each rack individually connected via its ground lug/stud. Green or green/yellow insulated conductors. Listed two-hole compression lugs on bare metal or with paint-piercing hardware. Doors, side panels and rails bonded. Trays and ladder racks bonded. Short, direct runs without coils or sharp bends.
  3. Continuity / bonding testMeasure resistance from each rack's frame to the busbar with a low-resistance ohmmeter or bonding tester. It should be very low (milliohms). A high reading means a loose, painted or corroded joint.
  4. Ground system resistanceThe electrician measures the grounding electrode system (fall-of-potential or clamp-on tester) against the design target. Many data centers aim for ≤ 5 Ω or lower.
  5. Power path checksConfirm PDU and receptacle grounds (outlet tester / PDU self-test). The PDU earth must tie to the same system.
  6. Record and repeatDocument readings, then recheck periodically and after moves/adds. Joints loosen and corrode.
Safety

Grounding ties into the building electrical system. Design, installation and earth-resistance testing are work for a qualified electrician under the local code. Never disconnect a ground on energised equipment.

Exam traps

  • "The power cord grounds the rack."It grounds that device's chassis. The rack frame, doors and trays need their own bonded path.
  • Daisy-chaining racks.Each rack taps the common conductor or busbar directly.
  • Bolting to paint.Paint and powder coat insulate. Use bare metal or paint-piercing washers.
  • Bonding vs grounding.Bonding = equal potential between metal parts. Grounding = connection of that system to earth. You need both.
  • Coiling extra conductor.Coils and sharp bends add impedance to fast surges. Keep runs short and direct.

Check yourself

1. What does bonding accomplish?

Bonding equalises potential between metal parts. Grounding then connects the bonded system to earth.

2. Why is daisy-chaining rack grounds a problem?

Each rack should connect independently to the busbar or row conductor.

3. A bond test from a rack frame to the busbar reads high. Most likely cause?

Bond resistance should be very low. High readings point to poor metal-to-metal contact.

4. Which standard covers bonding and grounding for telecom spaces?

TIA-607 defines the PBB/SBB/TBB hierarchy. TIA-942 adds data-center guidance.
